DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.09.3
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.09.2 and before will not load in this version: every stockpile figure is
   kept to a tenth now and there is a new building, so an old file describes a board this
   version cannot read. The Load screen says so.
7. This version ships for Windows and for Linux. On Linux, read RUN-ON-LINUX.txt in the
   folder instead of steps 2 to 5.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.09.2
-------------------------
Most of this version points off Earth: reasons to go far, and a way to fill a tank when
you get there. Eight rules moved, and the computer plays every one.

THE RULES THAT MOVED
- EVERY RESOURCE IS KEPT TO A TENTH. Materials, Fuel, Energy and Ducats show one decimal
  where they have one (a Bank pays 6.8, not 6). A price or an upkeep that was whole stays
  whole; nothing is rounded away any more.
- NUCLEAR ROCKETS, a new rung-2 Tech (cost 38), cut every transit by a fifth. Hardened
  Hulls now needs it as well as Efficient Transit.
- THE FIRST COLONY ON EACH WORLD CALMS EVERYONE. The first ground colony ever founded on a
  world takes half a point of Unrest off every country on Earth, whoever holds it.
- THE STADIUM, a new building, halves what the climate adds to a country's Unrest (the
  heat, the sea, a climate break or card). With a Constabulary as well, Unrest now moves
  in quarters.
- A COLONY WITH A WORKING REFINERY REFUELS SHIPS in its world's low orbit, from your
  stockpile, as a station does in its own orbit. A blockade of that orbit shuts it, and a
  ship that can reach one is no longer stranded.
- TRADE POSTS PAY BY DISTANCE. Each world you hold pays a Trade Post anywhere by how far
  it is from Earth: Earth 3 Ducats, the Moon 3.5, Venus 4, Mars 5, Phobos and Deimos 6.
- SHIPS BUILT ON THE MOON, PHOBOS OR DEIMOS COST LESS with a working mine there: three
  quarters of their materials with one mine, three fifths with two.
- THE COMPUTER COVETS A BIG COLONY. The more people and output a colony has, the more a
  computer side wants to take it, by influence or by landing an army.
- A CHOICE CARD THAT REACHES NOBODY goes back to the bottom of the deck and the next card
  is drawn; the report says why it passed each side by.

THE CARDS
- A SHIP'S OWN CARD CAN ATTACK. A frigate's, a battleship's or a missile carrier's card
  carries the stack's stance and its Attack button; the attack is still the whole stack's.
  Clicking empty space on the solar map closes a ship's card and brings back the roster.
- EVERY PLACE CARD SAYS ITS OUTPUT under its population, and a colony or station card its
  founding date under its name.
- THE COUNTRY CARD IS REARRANGED: Influence, Pioneers, Policies, the buildings, Armies,
  then Orders. A finished sea wall or scrubber is one line; hover it for the rest.
- THE BUILD HOVER USES GLYPHS for every figure, colonists included.
- END TURN NAMES EVERYTHING STILL OWED at once, and the Unrest lines in the report are
  quieter: one net line a country, with its causes named.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- GOING FAR: did Nuclear Rockets, the distance pay or a refinery colony make Mars or the
  small moons worth the trip?
- THE TENTHS: were the decimals easy to read, or noise?
- A BIG COLONY: if you built one place up, did the computer come for it? Did it feel fair?
- THE STADIUM: did you build one, and could you tell it was working?
- Anything that still says the same thing twice.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

PLAYING THE CUSTODIANS
----------------------
Your lever on Earth is switching industry off. Mothball a Factory, Mine, Power Plant or
Refinery, at home or in a country you have taken, and it emits nothing. Each Mothball
costs that country +1 Unrest.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. These are known. You do not need to report them.

WHAT THIS VERSION KNOWS ABOUT ITSELF

- THE WIN COLUMN: Custodians 7, Prospectors 5, Arkwrights 1, Archivists 7, where last
  version was 2 / 5 / 0 / 11. Most of the move came with the tenths.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 60 GAMES OF 80, where it was 62.
- THE COMPUTER ALMOST NEVER BUILDS A SHIPYARD ON THE MOON, PHOBOS OR DEIMOS, so the
  cheaper ships there are yours alone for now.
- THE COMPUTER'S APPETITE FOR A BIG COLONY is only visible when someone builds one; the
  computer sides do not build big colonies themselves, so the eighty games cannot show it.
- TWO STACKS THAT ATTACK IN ONE ORBIT IN ONE TURN FIGHT TWO BATTLES, one per attack.
- A COMMS BLACKOUT CANNOT STOP AN ATTACK YOU FOUGHT BEFORE THE TURN ENDED.
- AN ARKWRIGHTS PLAYER WHO OPENS IN MEXICO has six buildings in five slots and nothing free
  to build until an Industry Level raise.
- NOBODY CAN ACCEPT OR DECLINE AN ACCORD OFFERED TO THEM - INCLUDING YOU. The game still
  answers on your behalf.
- THE THREAT LINE ON A COUNTRY'S CARD STILL QUOTES THE FIRST EXCHANGE, while every other
  odds figure is the whole battle's.

CARRIED OVER, STILL TRUE

- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the same countries
  in the same order.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
