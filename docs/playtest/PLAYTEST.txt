DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.0
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. ON UBUNTU OR ANOTHER LINUX: take the "-linux" zip instead. There is no .exe in it;
   open a terminal in the unzipped folder and run   ./dying-earth   . The file
   RUN-ON-LINUX.txt beside it says what to do if the window does not open, and where
   the saves go. It is built on Ubuntu 22.04 and wants 22.04 or newer.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.07.6
-------------------------
The biggest version since the climate one. Education used to be a fixed number printed
on a region's card; now it is something you move, and half a dozen rules read it.

- SCHOOLS. A new building, one per region, that any side can build. While it stands it
  raises that region's Education Level by 0.25 a turn, up to 2.00, and the number falls
  back at the same rate if you mothball it. Off Earth the same building is called an
  Institute and goes on a colony or a station.
- EDUCATION NOW DOES THREE JOBS. It still multiplies what a Research Lab makes. It now
  also decides how much a region's PEOPLE add to that lab - so a school in a big,
  badly-schooled region is worth far more than one in a small, well-schooled one. And
  it makes a place harder for a RIVAL to buy: spend 30 Influence on a well-schooled
  region and only 27 of it sticks. Your own regions always convert in full.
- COLONISTS CARRY THEIR SCHOOLING WITH THEM. Emigrants take their home region's
  education level as it stands the turn they muster, and a colony's figure is the
  average of everyone who settled it.
- EVERY SIDE NOW HAS ONE BUILDING OF ITS OWN. It replaces a common building on your
  build list, at the same price, with its own name and picture:
    * Prospectors - INVESTMENT BANK, instead of the Bank. It pays 1% of your Venture
      Capital Fund back into the fund every turn, one per region, and it compounds.
      Your victory target moved from 750 to 1000 to go with it.
    * Arkwrights - SPACEPORT, instead of the Launch Site. +1 Influence for every
      emigrant it lifts OFF EARTH - onto a ship or to your station - paid into next
      turn's allotment. The sea to Antarctica pays nothing.
    * Archivists - REACTOR, instead of the Power Plant. While one is running you pay
      only 75% of the energy bill for everything you own, the Archive excepted.
    * Custodians - ACADEMY, instead of the School and the Institute. It teaches like a
      school and pays you 1 Ducat a turn wherever it stands.
  A captured one keeps working for whoever holds it. A plain building you capture does
  NOT turn into your version; you would have to knock it down and rebuild.
- A CONSTABULARY NOW DEFENDS AS WELL AS CALMS. While one stands, a rival needs your
  standing plus 25 to take that region instead of plus 20. It protects whoever holds
  the region, including someone who takes it off you.
- THE ARCHIVE IS NOW A PLACE YOU FILL. Four colonists must live at the colony before
  you may START it, and when it is finished you must UPLOAD twelve people into it from
  the people living there. Uploading is a free order; the people leave the living
  population for good, and the count can never go down. Four at a time is enough.
- RELATIONS. Every side now keeps a score of what it thinks of every other, on a grid
  at the foot of the Victory window (V). It drops a point each turn someone spends
  influence on a place you hold or attacks you, and recovers slowly. IT DOES NOTHING
  YET - it is there to be watched for a version before it is given teeth.
- THE REGION CARD SAYS WHAT A PLACE COSTS. Three lines under the standings: what the
  threshold is made of, what the holder's standing and the margin make of it, and what
  your influence is worth there.
- SENDING PEOPLE TO A SHIP GOT A BUTTON. A colony ship of yours parked at Earth now
  appears in the region card's Emigrants block beside the sea and station buttons.
- THE TECH TREE. Click the research figure or the race bar beside it to open it. Its
  legend used to draw itself through the middle of the tree; it is under the tree now.
  The window no longer opens underneath the Climate Panel.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > click a region on the globe, then Begin. If
  this is your first game, take the Custodians and tick "Play Tutorial" at the bottom of
  their card: you still choose where you start, and a short note opens each of the first
  five turns. The computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate
  button under the cards: the computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a region for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. M switches to the Solar System Map, Tab
  brings the roster back, and Esc goes back.
- Build things by clicking an empty box among a region's slots, or an empty tile among a
  colony's modules; a few things that take no slot have their own buttons on the card.
  Hover a build button to see what it makes and how long it takes. Hover almost any
  number: if a rule sets it, the rule is there.
- Spend Influence on other regions to win them over. Your home region starts held.
  Click a region and use Spend in the bottom-right corner, or Max to spend all of it.
- Every region has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the region throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the region they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (C). The coloured bar marks every point where the world changes
  for good, including where Antarctica opens. The top bar shows this turn's net
  pollution beside the temperature.
- Getting off Earth: muster Emigrants in a region with a Launch Site and send them
  straight to your station over Earth (a Habitat on it gives room for eight). Put a
  Shipyard on the station to build ships. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only
  in a launch window; hover Mars to see when. Open a colony's Hab View (M) to see what
  stands there and how much room is left.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- SCHOOLS: did you build one? Did you see the education number move, and did you work
  out what it was doing for you?
- YOUR OWN BUILDING: did you notice you had one? Did you understand what it did without
  hovering it?
- THE THREE LINES ON THE REGION CARD: do they read, or are they three numbers in a row?
  The middle one has a clause about the threshold that only appears sometimes.
- THE ARCHIVE, if you played the Archivists: did the four-people requirement stop you
  from starting it, and did you find the Upload button when it was finished?
- THE RELATIONS GRID: did you look at it? Did the fact that it does nothing bother you,
  or was it interesting to watch anyway?
- THE TECH TREE: did you try clicking the research number to open it?
- THE TUTORIAL TICK: did you find it on the Custodians' card? Did the five notes tell
  you enough to play on, or did you still not know what to do on turn six?
- THE INFLUENCE HOVERS: is there still a word in that section you could not explain?
- THE POPULATION NUMBERS: did "Region population" make sense? Did it bother you that
  Nigeria's region says 1.14 billion (it is all of sub-Saharan Africa)?
- THE LIFT TO A STATION: did you use it, and did it feel too easy?
- MAX: did you miss Defence?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE ARCHIVISTS ARE WINNING FAR TOO MUCH, AND WE KNOW WHY. Over eighty computer-only
  games they win 74, the Custodians 3, the Arkwrights 2 and the Prospectors none, and
  the game is usually over by turn sixteen. Making the Archive's target "twelve people
  uploaded" instead of "twelve people living beside it" was meant to make their job
  harder and made it much easier: uploads pile up across turns and each batch empties
  the station so the next can arrive. The number twelve was priced for the old, harder
  target. It is the next thing to fix. Tell us if the game ends before you have got
  going.
- Every side's victory target was set against a computer that never competed for it, so
  the numbers are all suspect.
- THE PROSPECTORS STILL WIN NOTHING. Their new Investment Bank compounds, but the games
  are ending too early for it to matter.
- THE HAB VIEW'S AND SLOT BOXES' PICTURES ARE FIRST PICKS from a free icon set and may
  change; say which ones misled you.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. Older than this version.
- THE SPACEPORT AND THE LAUNCH SITE look alike at the small size a build box draws them
  at: both are a tall thing over a low thing. Say so if you ever mistook one for the
  other.
- No sound.
