DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.09.8
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.09.7 and before will not load: the map has two more countries. The Load
   screen says so.
7. This version ships for Windows and for Linux. On Linux, read RUN-ON-LINUX.txt in the
   folder instead of steps 2 to 5.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.09.7
-------------------------
The Moon race version: the first landing on the Moon is a contest you can watch, fuel
follows real spaceflight, and the map has eighteen countries. The computer plays every
rule below.

THE SKY
- THE RACE TO THE MOON. Until somebody lands, the Moon's card lists how far each side has
  got: a Shipyard, a Colony Ship, people aboard, on the way, in orbit. You see it whether
  or not you can see their ships. The Report says when anyone sends a loaded Colony Ship
  there, and the landing gets a pop-up naming the winner and where everyone else stood.
- FUEL FOLLOWS REAL SPACEFLIGHT. Every trip costs what it would really cost, and the way
  out and the way back are priced separately:
    From Earth: the Moon 15.7, Venus 17.4, Mars 18.1, Phobos 19.7, Deimos 19.9.
    Home to Earth is cheap, because Earth's air does the braking: 3.7 from the Moon,
      12.1 from Mars.
    From the Moon everything is about half Earth's price: Mars is 9.0.
  A full tank (30, or 35 for a Colony Ship) reaches one place from Earth; plan to refuel.
- VENUS TO MARS is a trip you can make now, four turns at its own launch window.
- EARTH L4 AND EARTH L5: two new places for a station, far from Earth on its path round
  the Sun. You cannot build there from the ground or lift people to them. Fly a loaded
  Colony Ship there (16.3 Fuel) and found the station from the ship. They are on the
  Solar System map, not Earth's.
- A MASS DRIVER takes a quarter off the Fuel of every trip leaving its world, where it
  took a flat 4.

THE MAP
- EIGHTEEN COUNTRIES, up from sixteen. Iran now includes Pakistan and Afghanistan. Turkey
  is new, with Iraq, Syria, Israel, Jordan, Lebanon and the Caucasus. Kazakhstan is
  Central Asia on its own and has no coast, so the sea takes nothing from it. South
  Africa is new, split from Nigeria, with its neighbours up to Angola and Mozambique.
- THE CLIMATE IS KINDER, to make up for the extra countries' industry: the world absorbs
  8 ppm a turn, up from 6, and each half degree takes 330 ppm, up from 300.

THE RULES THAT MOVED
- RAISING A COUNTRY'S INDUSTRY LEVEL COSTS MORE EACH TIME: 30 Materials and 4 Widgets,
  then 40 and 5, then 50 and 6. The Prospectors pay half the Materials.
- THE PROSPECTORS' OPENING OBJECTIVE pays 50 Ducats, down from 75, and wants a working
  Investment Bank in three countries for three turns in a row. The Journal shows the
  count.

THE WINDOW
- RAISE INDUSTRY LEVEL IS A TILE, the last one among a country's building boxes, in red.
  Click it to order the raise; right-click to take it back. The button is gone.
- CARD HEADINGS ARE BIGGER, each with a small picture: Influence, Pioneers, Policies,
  Facilities, Armies, Orders.
- A COUNTRY'S CARD is shorter: "Region population 385.6 (386M)" and "GDP 23: Industry
  Level 3, leans Fuel". Hover the GDP line for what it pays.
- A COLONY'S CARD shows its people as a real number: "14/16 (14.2M)".
- THE TUTORIAL is about a seventh shorter.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE MOON RACE: did you watch it? Did it change what you did?
- FUEL: did you run a ship dry? Did you use the Moon as a stepping stone? Is the cheap
  trip home obvious?
- EARTH L4 AND L5: did you find them? Did you found a station there, and why?
- THE RAISE TILE: did you find it without the button?
- THE NEW COUNTRIES: is it clear who borders whom around Iran and Turkey?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. These are known. You do not need to report them.

WHAT THIS VERSION KNOWS ABOUT ITSELF

- THE WIN COLUMN: Custodians 9, Prospectors 27, Arkwrights 18, Archivists 7, where last
  version was 8 / 21 / 18 / 5. The Prospectors win too often; the kinder climate gave
  them most of the games it saved.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 17 GAMES OF 80, where it was 28. Fourteen of the 17 are games
  where the Prospectors go first and start in China.
- THE ARCHIVISTS SCORE NOTHING at the end in 53 games of 80.
- THE COMPUTER NEVER USES EARTH L4 OR L5, AND NEVER BUILDS A MASS DRIVER. Both are yours.
- THE COMPUTER ARKWRIGHTS reach the Moon late: they meet their objective in 29 games of
  80.
- A COMPUTER SIDE NOW STARTS IN SOUTH AFRICA, where one used to start in Australia.
- THE SLIDER FOR THE VENTURE FUND AND THE RESEARCH DIRECTIVE ON THE TOP BAR IS SHORT, so
  fine settings are easier in the Victory window and the Tech Tree.

CARRIED OVER, STILL TRUE

- THE COMPUTER BUILDS FEW REFINERIES OFF EARTH, so the better refining on the small moons is
  mostly yours.
- THE COMPUTER ALMOST NEVER BUILDS A SHIPYARD ON THE MOON, PHOBOS OR DEIMOS.
- TWO STACKS THAT ATTACK IN ONE ORBIT IN ONE TURN FIGHT TWO BATTLES, one per attack.
- NOBODY CAN ACCEPT OR DECLINE AN ACCORD OFFERED TO THEM - INCLUDING YOU. The game still
  answers on your behalf.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the same countries
  in the same order.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
