DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.07.5
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
   (Windows only again; there is no Linux zip for 0.07.5.)

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.07.4
-------------------------
One rule changed, and the rest is about reading the board. There is also a tutorial,
the first this game has ever had.

- A TUTORIAL. There is a Tutorial button on the title screen now. It starts an ordinary
  game as the Custodians and puts a short note at the start of each of the first five
  turns saying what that turn is for. Nothing is forced; the notes only say where to
  look, and after the fifth the game carries on as normal. Fair warning: the Custodians
  are the hardest of the four to win with. They are the side that teaches the climate.
- EVERY COLONY AND STATION NOW COMES WITH A CORE MODULE. It holds four people and costs
  one energy a turn, and it is what a place is founded with instead of a free habitat.
  The catch: a place has ONE BUILD SLOT FOR EACH PERSON LIVING THERE AND NONE BEFORE.
  A station you build this turn can hold four people at once but cannot build anything
  until they arrive. This is the only rule change, and it moved the balance a long way.
- MODULES ARE ON THE COLONY'S CARD NOW, drawn as tiles in the same grid a country's
  build slots use. The separate window is gone. Click a tile for its figures and
  buttons, an empty one to build. M no longer opens that window: it switches to the
  Solar System Map, the job Tab used to do. Tab brings the roster back.
- HOVER ANY WORD IN THE INFLUENCE SECTION of a country's card and it explains itself:
  what influence is, what a standing is, what a threshold is and what raises it, what
  blame is costing you, and how close your nearest rival is.
- THE GAME OPENS FULL SIZE, and the Climate Panel opens tall enough to read. It used to
  show about a third of itself.
- A POPULATION GRAPH. Hover the population figure in the top bar for Earth's people and
  space's, turn by turn. Every graph now labels its axis with dates rather than turn
  numbers.
- THE PICK A TECH BUTTON IS RED when it appears, like End Turn.
- EACH SIDE HAS A SYMBOL on the faction selection screen.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- Tutorial if this is your first game: it starts one for you and talks you through the
  first five turns. Otherwise New Game > pick one of the four sides > click a region on
  the globe, then Begin. The computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate
  button under the cards: the computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a region for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. M switches to the Solar System Map, Tab
  brings the roster back, and Esc goes back.
- Build things by clicking an empty box among a region's slots, or an empty tile among a
  colony's modules; a few things that take no slot have their own buttons on the card.
  Hover a build button to see what it makes and how long it takes. Hover almost any
  number: if a rule sets it, the rule is there.
- Spend Influence on other regions to win them over. Your home region starts held.
  Click a region and use Spend in the bottom-right corner, or Max to spend all of it.
- Every region has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the region throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the region they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (C). The coloured bar marks every point where the world changes
  for good, including where Antarctica opens. The top bar shows this turn's net
  pollution beside the temperature.
- Getting off Earth: muster Emigrants in a region with a Launch Site and send them
  straight to your station over Earth (a Habitat on it gives room for eight). Put a
  Shipyard on the station to build ships. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only
  in a launch window; hover Mars to see when. Open a colony's Hab View (M) to see what
  stands there and how much room is left.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE TUTORIAL: did you use it? Did the five notes tell you enough to play on, or did
  you still not know what to do on turn six?
- THE CORE MODULE: did "one build slot per person" make sense on the card, or did you
  find out by being refused?
- THE INFLUENCE HOVERS: is there still a word in that section you could not explain?
- THE POPULATION GRAPH: did the two scales confuse you?
- THE POPULATION NUMBERS: did "Region population" make sense? Did it bother you that
  Nigeria's region says 1.14 billion (it is all of sub-Saharan Africa)?
- THE LIFT TO A STATION: did you use it, and did it feel too easy?
- MAX: did you miss Defence?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE BALANCE MOVED A LOT and none of it has been played by a person yet. Over eighty
  computer-only games the Archivists now win 46, the Custodians 16, the Arkwrights 1
  and the Prospectors none, with the world collapsing in 17. Last version it was
  Custodians 36, Archivists 21. The core module did that: a station over Earth counts
  as off Earth and can be lived on from the day it is built, which is most of what the
  Archivists need. Tell us if any side feels hopeless.
- Every side's victory target was set against a computer that never competed for it, so
  the numbers are all suspect.
- THE HAB VIEW'S AND SLOT BOXES' PICTURES ARE FIRST PICKS from a free icon set and may
  change; say which ones misled you.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. Older than this version.
- No sound, no tutorial beyond the first Report.
