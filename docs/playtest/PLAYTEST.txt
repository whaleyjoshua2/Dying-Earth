DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.06.0
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
   (There is a separate Linux zip: unzip it, then run ./dying-earth from the folder.)

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.05.5
-------------------------
This is the space version. The big one first:
- Every ship now carries its own FUEL TANK (30). A ship is built with it full, a flight
  spends from it, and it is refilled only by a "Refuel" order at a body where you have a
  space station. A ship whose tank cannot pay any flight is stranded until you build a
  station in orbit there. The Solar System Map shows "tank 20/30" on every stack.
- VENUS is on the map: no ground to land on, three station slots, three turns and 16 Fuel
  from Earth on its own launch window (hover Venus to see it). Everything at Venus is
  built and held in orbit, and people living on a Venus station count as off Earth.
- Stations over Earth now count as off Earth too: people living on the ISS count for
  Off-world Presence, and the Archive may stand there.
- New buildings: the OBSERVATORY makes Research on a colony or station (1% more per
  colonist living there); the SOLAR ARRAY on a station makes Energy that follows the
  sunlight (twice Earth's at Venus, under half at Mars); the MASS DRIVER on the Moon,
  Phobos or Deimos (needs Efficient Transit) makes your departures 4 Fuel cheaper and
  its mines richer. Habitats hold 8 people now.
- Modules are cheaper at a colony that already has a working mine (x0.75 with one,
  x0.6 with two or more). A Trade Post pays for the shape of your empire: 2 Ducats per
  colonist at its body plus 3 for every other body you hold, one per body.
- The hotter Earth gets, the more a Colony Ship will take beyond its capacity (one more
  per 0.2 C above +1.8, up to four), and each of those extra people may die on the way.
  The Load panel tells you the safe load and the crowded one.
- Every side's win now waits on a rung-3 tech of its own (Planetary Stewardship, the
  Extraction Charter, Generation Ships, the Upload), each also a real tech for everyone.
- The sides were retuned: the Custodians' Influence eases to 1.2 and an idle factory of
  theirs on Earth doubles a mine of theirs off it (Production Moved); the Prospectors'
  countries pay a fifth more Ducats and the market sells to them at 15% off; the
  Arkwrights' ships cost 15% less; the Archivists lost their output penalty and research
  at 1.5 on Earth and 1.75 off it.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > pick a starting continent. The computer plays
  the other three. There is also a Spectate button under the cards: the computer plays
  all four and you just watch, which is a quick way to see what a whole game looks like.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a country for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. Tab switches to the Solar System Map;
  Esc goes back.
- Build things with the buttons on a card. Hover a build button to see what it makes.
- Spend Influence on other countries to win them over. Your home country starts held.
- Every country has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the country throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull carbon
  back out of the air, and they calm the country they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (the C key). The coloured bar across it marks five points where
  the world changes for good - the reefs die, the permafrost thaws, and so on. Under it,
  "Last turn to act" is the last turn on which stopping all emissions would still save you.
- Getting off Earth: you start with a space station over Earth (except the Arkwrights, who
  build one). Put a Shipyard on it first - ships are only built at a Shipyard. Muster
  Emigrants in a country with a Launch Site, lift them, and load them on a Colony Ship.
  The Moon is one turn away and is where most colonies go. Mars is cheap only in a launch
  window; hover Mars to see when.
- Antarctica has three colony sites on Earth itself, but they stay under the ice until the
  world has warmed to +1.6 C. After that, Emigrants can be sent there by sea.
- The game saves itself every three turns and when it ends, and there is a Save button in
  the top bar you can press at the start of any turn. Saves live in
  %LOCALAPPDATA%\DyingEarth\data\saves - the Load screen has a button that opens that
  folder for you. Load a game from the title screen. A 0.05 save will not load.
- Some turns stop for a "Moment": one big sentence about something that just happened. At
  the bottom of each turn's Report there are tick boxes to switch any of them off.
- End Turn (top left, red). Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature in the top bar:
  at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.
- The Tech Tree, Climate Panel, Victory and Trading buttons open panels that explain the rest.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- A rule you could not find explained anywhere.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did. Thirty-six turns is new: did it drag?
- The turn you felt you had lost, and why.
- Did you get anyone to Mars? Was the launch window clear enough to plan around?
- Did the Emigrants make sense - where they wait, how they get on a ship, the send to
  Antarctica?
- If you played the Prospectors: did the Fund's share feel like a real choice?
- Did you lose your home country to the computer, and did the orange warning give you
  time to do anything about it?
- Whether the big line at the top of each turn's Report was the right thing to put there,
  or whether something more important was buried below it.
- Whether the Moments interrupted too much, and whether you switched any of them off.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- The sides are not balanced against each other. In our own computer-only test games
  the Custodians win almost every game that does not collapse (96 of 160) and reach their
  own win around turn 27; the Prospectors never reach 750 in the Fund (they get to about
  450); the Archivists finished the Archive in a handful of games and won one.
- The computer Custodians take other sides' home countries in most games, around turn
  19 to 23, and nobody takes theirs.
- Some seatings run hot: starting as the Custodians in East Asia, or as the Prospectors
  from either continent, the world collapsed in 18 to 20 of 20 computer-only games. From
  Europe the Custodians hold it.
- The computer builds Solar Arrays over Earth by the dozen, and Sea Walls when it has the
  tech, sometimes instead of Scrubbers.
- The computer never researches Efficient Transit on purpose, so you will rarely see it
  build a Mass Driver; the computer Prospectors never fly to Venus; and a computer side
  sometimes flies a loaded ship to Venus before its station there is up, and the ship sits
  stranded until it is.
- A ship stranded far from any station of yours is stranded for good unless you build one
  there. Check the tank before you fly.
- No sound, no tutorial beyond the first Report.
