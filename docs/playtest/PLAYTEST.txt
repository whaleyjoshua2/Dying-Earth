DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.1
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. ON UBUNTU OR ANOTHER LINUX: take the "-linux" zip instead. There is no .exe in it;
   open a terminal in the unzipped folder and run   ./dying-earth   . The file
   RUN-ON-LINUX.txt beside it says what to do if the window does not open, and where
   the saves go. It is built on Ubuntu 22.04 and wants 22.04 or newer.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.0
-------------------------
Mostly this version is about KNOWING WHO. Last version gave every side its own building
and started keeping a grudge score; this one gives you somewhere to read all of it, and
puts a name and a badge on the things that belong to somebody.

- A FACTION WINDOW. Press F, or the new button on the top bar. A dropdown in its top
  right picks any of the four sides. It shows their symbol large, how far along their
  victory they are, what they earned last turn, their share of the pollution, what they
  think of the other three and what the other three think of them, and what they hold.
  Underneath, folded away until you click it, is their CARD - the one you read on the
  setup screen and could never get back to once a game started: their multipliers, their
  signature rule, their own building and their victory condition.
  You see a rival's income as totals only. The building-by-building breakdown is yours
  alone. The Relations grid has moved off the Victory window into here.
- SHIPS HAVE NAMES. Every ship is christened when it is built - TSV Magellan, ARK
  Endurance, PMV Valiant - drawn from two lists, explorers for colony ships and warships
  for the rest. The three letters in front are your side's. The old "Colony Ship 3" is
  still there if you hover it.
- BADGES ON WHAT IS YOURS. Your side's symbol now appears at the head of a colony or
  station's card, the way a nation's flag sits on a region card, and beside every ship.
  A place nobody holds wears none.
- THE TUTORIAL GETS TO THE POINT. It used to spend two of its five notes telling you
  things and not asking for anything, so the first instruction landed on turn three and
  "get off Earth" on turn five. Now the first three turns each ask for ONE thing, and the
  three are one chain: spend your influence, muster emigrants, send them to the ISS.
  Turns four and five still appear but ask for nothing - they point at the climate and at
  the research race.
- A STATION CAN ASK FOR PEOPLE. You could always send emigrants UP to a station from a
  region's card. Now the station's own card has a "from <region>" dropdown and a Lift
  button, and an Antarctic colony has the same thing for the sea. Both ends, same order.
- HABITATS ARE SMALLER AGAIN. A Habitat holds 4 people, down from 8. EXPANDED HABITATS
  now adds 4 instead of 2, so a researched Habitat holds the 8 it used to - which means
  the squeeze is on the EARLY game, before you have the tech. The Core Module every
  station and colony starts with still holds 4 flat.
- THE ARCHIVE MUST LEAVE EARTH ORBIT. If you play the Archivists, you can no longer raise
  the Archive on your station over Earth. It has to stand on another body - the Moon
  counts, and so does anywhere further. This is the biggest rule change in the version
  and it has cut the Archivists hard; see the rough edges below.
- AN EIGHTEENTH TECH, AND A DEARER FIRST RUNG. Rung-1 techs cost 18 instead of 15 and
  Coastal Engineering 14 instead of 12. CIVIL DEFENSE is new, on the Society branch
  behind Public Science: while it stands, a Constabulary makes a region cost a rival your
  standing plus 30 instead of plus 25.
- SCHOOLS ARE SLOWER, AND YOU CAN FINALLY SEE THEM WORK. The step is 0.20 a turn instead
  of 0.25. More to the point: the Education Level on a region's card used to be the
  number printed in the rulebook and never moved, whatever your school did. It is now the
  LIVE figure, and hovering it tells you what the card says and what your school has
  added.
- SMALLER THINGS YOU READ. The bottom-right command corner is 15% bigger. "Emigrants
  waiting" now sits directly above the Muster button and shows even when it is zero. The
  research race bar has moved onto the top of the Tech Tree window, with the sentence it
  replaced on its hover. Hovering a "Found a Colony" button tells you what the site is
  worth. And the race bar itself used to under-draw - research carried over from the last
  tech belongs to nobody, and was simply not painted; it is grey now, so the bar adds up.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > click a region on the globe, then Begin. If
  this is your first game, take the Custodians and tick "Play Tutorial" at the bottom of
  their card: you still choose where you start, and a short note opens each of the first
  five turns. The computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate button under
  the cards: the computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a region for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. M switches to the Solar System Map, Tab
  brings the roster back, and Esc goes back. F opens the Faction window.
- Build things by clicking an empty box among a region's slots, or an empty tile among a
  colony's modules; a few things that take no slot have their own buttons on the card.
  Hover a build button to see what it makes and how long it takes. Hover almost any
  number: if a rule sets it, the rule is there.
- Spend Influence on other regions to win them over. Your home region starts held.
  Click a region and use Spend in the bottom-right corner, or Max to spend all of it.
- Every region has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the region throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the region they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (C). The coloured bar marks every point where the world changes
  for good, including where Antarctica opens. The top bar shows this turn's net
  pollution beside the temperature.
- Getting off Earth: muster Emigrants in a region with a Launch Site - your starting
  region always has one - and send them straight to your station over Earth, either from
  the region's card or from the station's own. Put a Shipyard on the station to build
  ships. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only in a launch window; hover Mars to
  see when.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE FACTION WINDOW: did you find it? Did you open a rival's page, and did anything on
  it change what you did next? Was the folded-away card worth unfolding?
- SHIP NAMES: did you notice them? Did losing "Colony Ship 3" ever leave you unsure
  which ship you were ordering?
- THE TUTORIAL, if you used it: the first three turns each ask for one thing. Did you do
  them? Did turn four leave you knowing what to do on turn six?
- THE ARCHIVE, if you played the Archivists: you have to get to the Moon before you can
  start it. Did you manage it, and roughly what turn? Did the game end before you
  finished?
- HABITATS AT 4: did the early game feel tight for room? Did you notice Expanded
  Habitats being worth more than it used to be?
- THE EDUCATION LEVEL ON A REGION'S CARD: build a school and watch it. Does it move
  visibly enough to be worth the 25 materials?
- CIVIL DEFENSE: did you research it? Did you already have a Constabulary when you did?
- THE BIGGER COMMAND CORNER: better, or does it crowd the card above it?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE SIDES HAVE SWAPPED PLACES, AND WE KNOW WHY. Over eighty computer-only games the
  Custodians now win 42, the Prospectors 11, the Arkwrights 7 and the Archivists 6. Last
  version the Archivists won 58 and the Arkwrights none. Almost all of that is the one
  rule that stopped the Archive standing in Earth orbit: their Archive now goes up around
  turn 25 instead of turn 19, which leaves too few turns to upload twelve people into it.
  The table is more even than it has ever been and no side is on nothing, but the
  Custodians at 42 are now the problem the Archivists used to be. Tell us if a game felt
  decided early.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES MORE OFTEN - 14 games in 80, against 1 last version. This is not a
  climate change: nothing about pollution moved. It is that the Archivists used to END
  games around turn 20 by winning them, and now nobody does, so the world gets fifteen
  more turns to heat up. Whether that is a fair game or a grim one is exactly what we
  want to hear about.
- THE TECH TREE STILL FINISHES. Rung 1 got dearer and a tech was added, and a typical
  game still researches all eighteen with turns to spare, so research never really
  constrains you. Making it bite means moving the dearer rungs, which we have not done.
- THE ARKWRIGHTS STILL CANNOT REALLY WIN. Their victory wants 30 colonists spread over
  three bodies. In eighty games no side has EVER founded a colony in the Mars system, on
  Venus, or on Phobos or Deimos - only the Moon is ever settled. Their condition is
  close to impossible rather than merely hard.
- EVERY SIDE'S VICTORY TARGET was set against a computer that never competed for it, so
  the numbers are all still suspect.
- HOVERING A "FOUND A COLONY" BUTTON should tell you the site's four yields. We have not
  been able to photograph it, so it is the one new thing in this version nobody has
  actually looked at. Tell us if it is missing or wrong.
- THE HAB VIEW'S AND SLOT BOXES' PICTURES ARE FIRST PICKS from a free icon set and may
  change; say which ones misled you.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. Older than this version.
- THE SPACEPORT AND THE LAUNCH SITE look alike at the small size a build box draws them
  at: both are a tall thing over a low thing. Say so if you ever mistook one for the
  other.
- No sound.
