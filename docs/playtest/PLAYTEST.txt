DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.07.1
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
   (Windows only again; there is no Linux zip for 0.07.1.)

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.07.0
-------------------------
This one is almost all about reading the board and reaching its controls. Nothing about
how the game is won has changed, and only one number moved.

- EVERYTHING THE GAME COUNTS NOW HAS A PICTURE, AND THE PICTURES HAVE COLOURS. People,
  influence and pollution had none; they are now a person, a megaphone and a chimney.
  Materials and Ducats swapped pictures, because the old ones were the wrong way round:
  the "money" one was labelled Materials and the "mineral" one was labelled Ducats. All
  eight now have a colour of their own, so you can pick a figure out of a line without
  reading it - the red jerrycan is fuel, the brown chimney is pollution.
- THE CONTROLS YOU USE EVERY TURN NOW LIVE IN ONE CORNER. Bottom right, under the panel:
  how much Influence you have left, a Spend button for whatever you have clicked,
  a Defence button, and End Turn. End Turn has MOVED there from the top of the screen.
- THE DEFENCE BUTTON spreads your unspent Influence across the places a rival could
  actually take, most threatened first. It funds a place all the way to safe or not at
  all, because holding a place is a threshold and not a race - half-defending two places
  loses both. Tick "every turn" and it does the same each turn, as ordinary orders you
  can read and cancel before you end the turn. It never spends behind your back. The
  computer players defend the same way now.
- INFLUENCE IS THE FIRST THING ON A COUNTRY'S CARD, not the last. It used to be below
  the buildings, the armies and two paragraphs of help.
- THE ROSTER ON THE RIGHT IS SORTED AND SHORTER. Each heading says how many rows still
  want an order from you, and clicking that number hides everything else in that group.
  Armies no longer nag: an army keeps the stance you gave it until you move it, and the
  row now shows which stance that is.
- THE COLONY SITE NUMBERS ON A WORLD MAP ARE PICTURES NOW. "M 1.22 G 0.80 R 1.86 H 1.63"
  was four letters nobody was ever taught; it is now a cart, a bolt, a can and a person,
  which is what a mine, a generator, a refinery and a habitat would make there.
- NINE MORE THINGS EXPLAIN THEMSELVES WHEN YOU POINT AT THEM. The rule is: if the game
  shows you a bare number that a rule governs, pointing at it tells you the rule. Unrest
  and its three thresholds; what a Standing is and how fast it fades; how a country's
  building slots are set; what a building's upkeep and pollution actually do; a colony's
  size limit; a ship's tank and what "stranded" means.
- TWO SIDES CHANGED COLOUR. The Arkwrights are a darker purple and the Archivists are
  crimson instead of pale blue. This was to keep side colours and figure colours from
  being confused with each other. Map labels are also drawn lighter, so they read against
  their own territory.
- RESEARCH COSTS A TENTH MORE. The only balance change. It is a nudge: measured over
  eighty computer-only games it costs the tech tree four completions out of eighty and
  makes a game two or three turns longer.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > pick a starting continent, from the list or by
  clicking the globe. The computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate button
  under the cards: the computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a country for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. Tab switches to the Solar System Map;
  Esc goes back.
- Build things with the buttons on a card. Hover a build button to see the cost and what
  it makes. Hover almost any number now: if a rule sets it, the rule is there.
- Spend Influence on other countries to win them over. Your home country starts held.
  Click a country and use the Spend button in the bottom-right corner, or the one on its
  card. Press Defence to spread what is left over the places you might lose.
- Every country has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the country throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the country they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (the C key). The coloured bar marks every point where the world
  changes for good, including where Antarctica opens. The top bar now also shows this
  turn's net pollution beside the temperature, so you can see at a glance whether the
  world went over or under the natural sink.
- Getting off Earth: put a Shipyard on your station first - ships are only built at a
  Shipyard. Muster Emigrants in a country with a Launch Site, lift them, and load them on
  a Colony Ship. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only in a launch window; hover
  Mars to see when. Remember a colony's size limit: land people before you plan buildings.
- End Turn - BOTTOM RIGHT now, red. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature in the top
  bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- A rule you could not find explained anywhere - there should be far fewer of those now.
- DO THE PICTURES READ? Can you tell the eight apart at a glance, in the top bar and in a
  list of buildings? Is any one of them a puzzle? The chimney is the one we are least
  sure of: at the size the bar draws it, you mostly see the smoke.
- DID YOU FIND THE BOTTOM-RIGHT CORNER? End Turn moved there. Did you look for it at the
  top first, and for how long?
- DOES DEFENCE DO WHAT YOU EXPECT? It deliberately does nothing when nobody can take
  anything from you, and it deliberately refuses to half-fund a place. Did it ever leave
  Influence unspent when you wanted it used?
- Did "every turn" feel safe, or did you want to see what it was going to do first?
- CAN YOU DRAG THE CLIMATE PANEL SMALLER? Still the one change nobody has watched work.
- Two sides changed colour. If you played 0.07.0, did that trip you up?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE CUSTODIANS ARE TOO STRONG. Over eighty computer-only games they win 69, from any
  seat. That is the next thing to fix and this version deliberately did not touch it.
- THE ARCHIVISTS WIN NOTHING in those eighty, though the Archive itself gets built and
  finished. Getting twelve people to live at it is the part that fails.
- Every side's victory target was set against a computer that never competed for it, so
  the numbers are all suspect.
- RESEARCH IS NOT A CONSTRAINT. The whole tech tree gets finished in 75 of 80 games with
  six turns or more to spare. Whether it should is a question for the next version.
- DEFENCE ONLY REACTS AT THE LAST MOMENT. It answers a rival who could take a place next
  turn, and nobody sooner - so somebody who jumps a long way in one turn is never
  answered in time. Known, and not yet decided what to do about it.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. That is older than this version, and not caused by the new pictures.
- No sound, no tutorial beyond the first Report.
