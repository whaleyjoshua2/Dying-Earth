DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.2
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. ON UBUNTU OR ANOTHER LINUX: take the "-linux" zip instead. There is no .exe in it;
   open a terminal in the unzipped folder and run   ./dying-earth   . The file
   RUN-ON-LINUX.txt beside it says what to do if the window does not open, and where
   the saves go. It is built on Ubuntu 22.04 and wants 22.04 or newer.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.1
-------------------------
Last version gave you somewhere to READ what the other three sides think of you. This one
makes it matter. The grudge score has a cause, a vocabulary, a memory and a use, and for
the first time you can strike a bargain with somebody.

- SIDES NOW MIND YOUR POLLUTION. Your share of the four sides' pollution no longer only
  makes neutral regions dearer to win over: the other three hold it against you, each by
  its own measure. The Custodians mind twice as much as the Archivists, the Arkwrights
  half as much, and the Prospectors do not mind at all. Play dirty and you lose the room
  to deal as well as the ground.
- THE SCORE READS AS WORDS. Friendly, Cordial, Neutral, Wary, Cold, Hostile. Hover one and
  you get the number, and how much of it is your deeds and how much is your smoke.
- OFFENCES NOW DIFFER IN SIZE. Bidding on one region they hold is not the same as taking
  it, or as opening a battle. A turn charges everything you did in it, up to a limit -
  and it charges per PLACE, so splitting one spend across three orders costs you no more
  than one.
- A GRUDGE CAN SCAR. Cross the same side on enough turns and the best that pair can ever
  recover to drops, permanently. Only an Accord kept lifts it again.
- IT BITES THE BOARD. A side that thinks badly of you defends its places against you a
  little harder than against anybody else.
- ACCORDS. New. In the Faction window, on another side's page, you can offer them a
  bargain: NON-AGGRESSION (neither of you spends influence on the other's places or opens
  a battle), PASSAGE, REFUELLING at each other's stations, or - if you are Friendly - a
  RESEARCH AGREEMENT that pays you both a tenth more research. They can offer you one
  too, and a refusal costs nothing. Ending an Accord is free and takes a turn's notice.
  BREAKING one - acting against it while it still stands - costs you and ends the whole
  bargain at once.
- TRIBUTE. A fixed gift of Ducats or Materials to another side, once a turn. It and a
  kept Accord are the only two things in the game that RAISE what somebody thinks of you.
- MARKET PRICES MOVE. Every price in the trading window went up by one and is now the
  middle of a small band rather than a fixed number. Enough buying across the table in a
  turn pushes a price up a step, enough selling pushes it down, and it drifts home only
  on a turn nobody traded that good at all.
- THINGS YOU READ. Every ship now has its own line in the roster, by name, instead of one
  line for a whole stack. A ship in transit is named on the solar map. A colony or
  station's card wears your badge alone at its head - the little "what kind of thing is
  this" mark has gone from card headings, though it stays in the roster. An empty build
  box says "Click to Build". The "Found a Colony" button now shows the site's four yields
  on its face instead of on a hover. The research race bar on the Tech Tree says who has
  put in what, by name and percentage. And the faction-select screen's four cards are the
  same height with their buttons in a row, with a scrollbar when they do not fit.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > click a region on the globe, then Begin. If
  this is your first game, take the Custodians and tick "Play Tutorial" at the bottom of
  their card: you still choose where you start, and a short note opens each of the first
  five turns. The computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate button under
  the cards: the computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a region for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. M switches to the Solar System Map, Tab
  brings the roster back, and Esc goes back. F opens the Faction window.
- Build things by clicking an empty box among a region's slots, or an empty tile among a
  colony's modules; a few things that take no slot have their own buttons on the card.
  Hover a build button to see what it makes and how long it takes. Hover almost any
  number: if a rule sets it, the rule is there.
- Spend Influence on other regions to win them over. Your home region starts held.
  Click a region and use Spend in the bottom-right corner, or Max to spend all of it.
- Every region has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the region throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the region they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (C). The coloured bar marks every point where the world changes
  for good, including where Antarctica opens. The top bar shows this turn's net
  pollution beside the temperature.
- Getting off Earth: muster Emigrants in a region with a Launch Site - your starting
  region always has one - and send them straight to your station over Earth, either from
  the region's card or from the station's own. Put a Shipyard on the station to build
  ships. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only in a launch window; hover Mars to
  see when.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE FACTION WINDOW: did you find it? Did you open a rival's page, and did anything on
  it change what you did next? Was the folded-away card worth unfolding?
- SHIP NAMES: did you notice them? Did losing "Colony Ship 3" ever leave you unsure
  which ship you were ordering?
- THE TUTORIAL, if you used it: the first three turns each ask for one thing. Did you do
  them? Did turn four leave you knowing what to do on turn six?
- THE ARCHIVE, if you played the Archivists: you have to get to the Moon before you can
  start it. Did you manage it, and roughly what turn? Did the game end before you
  finished?
- HABITATS AT 4: did the early game feel tight for room? Did you notice Expanded
  Habitats being worth more than it used to be?
- THE EDUCATION LEVEL ON A REGION'S CARD: build a school and watch it. Does it move
  visibly enough to be worth the 25 materials?
- CIVIL DEFENSE: did you research it? Did you already have a Constabulary when you did?
- THE BIGGER COMMAND CORNER: better, or does it crowd the card above it?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE CUSTODIANS STILL WIN TOO MUCH. Over eighty computer-only games they take 37, the
  Prospectors 10, the Archivists 7 and the ARKWRIGHTS 3. Nothing in this version was
  aimed at the balance and none of it explains the shift; the Arkwrights have halved and
  are now the side to worry about. We know. It is a job for a later version.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES MORE OFTEN - 23 games in 80 against 14 last version - AND THAT IS
  DELIBERATE. This is a game about getting off Earth before the climate wins; a world
  that bites is the point. The likeliest cause is that everything in the trading window
  costs one more, so there is less money for scrubbers and leapfrogs. Tell us whether a
  losing game felt like a race you lost or like a foregone conclusion.
- NEARLY HALF OF ALL PAIRS END A GAME SCARRED. Between 42% and 47% of the twelve
  relationships carry a permanent floor by the end, and a third to a half sit at Cold or
  worse. The scale still says something - a typical pair ends a point or four below
  neutral - but if everyone ends up hating everyone the score stops meaning much. Say if
  it felt that way.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION EVER GETS STRUCK. The computer sides offer and accept that one and
  nothing else, because passage and refuelling are worth almost nothing on today's board:
  in eighty games no warship ever sat on a rival's station slot, and nobody needs to
  refuel at somebody else's station when everyone has their own over Earth. Both terms
  are built for when the game reaches Mars.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides only ever buy Materials
  and NEVER sell anything. So those two prices move only when YOU trade them. Giving the
  computer an appetite to sell is a later job.
- THE TECH TREE STILL FINISHES, now around turn 31 to 36 rather than the mid-game, so
  research bites a little more than it did but still runs out before the game does.
- TWO NEW THINGS NOBODY HAS LOOKED AT. Hovering a side's line in the "in orbit" list
  should name their ships; and the "Found a Colony" button should carry the site's four
  yields on its face. Both are built and neither could be photographed, because our
  screenshot tool cannot hold a mouse pointer still. Tell us if either is missing or
  wrong.
- THE HAB VIEW'S AND SLOT BOXES' PICTURES ARE FIRST PICKS from a free icon set and may
  change; say which ones misled you.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. Older than this version.
- THE SPACEPORT AND THE LAUNCH SITE look alike at the small size a build box draws them
  at: both are a tall thing over a low thing. Say so if you ever mistook one for the
  other.
- No sound.
