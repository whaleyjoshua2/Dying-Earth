DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.8
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.08.7 and before will not load in this version: the rules of war changed
   under them; the Load screen says so. This version ships for Windows only.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.7
-------------------------
The war. Last version let you read it; this one lets you fight it, on the ground and in
orbit. Every rule below the computer plays too.

- YOUR COUNTRY'S OWN ARMY CAN MARCH. Last version kept a country's standing army at home
  for good. Now you may march it like any other; at home it defends with its people, away
  from home it is an ordinary army and a threat to whoever it stands next to. The computer
  marches its own last and its raised armies first.
- ARMIES MOVE AS A STACK. Under the stance row on a country card, one row of buttons moves
  every army there at once ("All 2 (8): attack Russia"), with the odds for the whole stack
  on hover; the per-army rows stay if you want to split. "Repair all" too. Ships get the
  same: "All 2 that can" per destination. THE COMPUTER MASSES NOW: two armies together
  clear odds one alone would not, and it knows it.
- CLICK A SHIELD, THEN RIGHT-CLICK THE MAP. Clicking your own army shield arms the stack:
  a ring on the shield, the countries it can reach outlined. Right-click one of them to
  march the whole stack there; right-click it again to take the order back. Escape, End
  Turn or a click elsewhere disarms. Works for ships on the Solar System Map too.
- PASSAGE IS A REAL RULE. Under a Passage bargain your armies may march into the other
  side's countries without attacking; they sit on Hold, fight nobody and defend nothing
  until the bargain ends, then they are attacking. The computer offers it with
  non-aggression when it is Cordial with you and shares a border.
- THE BATTERY: a new building for a colony or a station (25 Materials, 3 Energy a turn).
  It fights in orbit with strength 4 and 6 hit points, never runs, and while it stands
  NOBODY ELSE HOLDS ORBITAL CONTROL there: no landing against you, no blockade of your
  station. It does not give you control either; a fleet that wants your ground has to
  shoot it down first. Repair it with Materials on its tile. The computer builds one when a
  rival warship parks over its colony.
- BOMBARD: a battleship holding an orbit outright (nobody else's warships, no enemy
  Battery) can bombard a rival colony there from the Ship card. One building, drawn at
  random, has a 25% chance to burn; if it is a Habitat the people beyond the room left
  die with it. It is a rung-3 offence like starting a battle, and it is never allowed over
  Earth. The computer does it when it has cause against you.
- ESCORTS WORK. While you have a warship in a battle, hits land on the warships; a colony
  ship or carrier beside them is not hit and not chased. Before, a colony ship next to a
  frigate took half the hits.
- BIG FIGHTS ARE DEADLIER. A battle in orbit rolls for a hit once a round per warship or
  Battery present (never fewer than three), instead of a flat three however many hulls
  were there. A battleship can now die in a turn if enough guns are on it.
- REFUEL AT A FRIEND'S STATION. Under a Refuel bargain you can fill your tanks at the
  other side's station, from your own stockpile. A ship at a body with only a partner's
  station is no longer stranded. The computer offers it where it has ships and no station
  of its own.
- CARRIERS AND INTERCEPT FOR EVERYONE. Any side with a grudge against a colony off Earth
  may build a carrier and lift an army to it, not only the Prospectors; and Intercept
  fires now against unarmed ships arriving, without needing orbital control.
- BATTLES IN ORBIT ARE MARKED ON THE MAP. The ring is gone; one crossed-swords mark in the
  attacker's colour sits beside every place a battle was fought last turn, on Earth, on a
  body and in orbit on the Solar System Map, with a "Battles last turn" list there.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Set the amount on the slider at the bottom right
  and press Spend, or Max.
- ARMIES are raised from a region's card and march to a neighbouring region from the
  Armies list on that card. Arriving in a region you do not hold is an attack; hover the
  button first. A region's own standing army can march too, but away from home it
  loses its people's defence. Warships are built at a
  shipyard and fight in orbit; a warship in a rival station's slot can be ordered to
  Blockade.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  what they have under way, what bargains stand between them, where you smear them, and
  where you request carbon credits. Your own page is where you greenwash.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider (and the Prospectors' Fund slider).
- The TRADING window (R) buys and sells goods.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - the sun at the bottom right, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the
  temperature in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE ATTACK HOVER: before you attacked, did you know who you were fighting and what the
  number meant? Was six lines too many?
- THE THREAT LINE: did it warn you in time? Did you want to know the army's orders, or
  was "it is there" enough?
- THE BATTLE RING: did you notice it? Did you find the Report from it? Did you miss a
  mark for battles in orbit?
- ARMY ORDERS IN THE ARMIES LIST: better than the bottom of the card? Is the block the
  right size?
- STANCE HOVERS: did you hover them? Did you use Intercept once you knew what it did?
- OFFLINE BUILDINGS: did you notice one go dim? Was the cause on the hover enough?
- THE SUN in its own column: still obvious?
- Anything that still says the same thing twice.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first.

- THE WIN COLUMN BARELY MOVED: Custodians 43, Prospectors 22, Arkwrights 1, Archivists 0
  (last version 43 / 21 / 2 / 0). The ground war is wide open now - 347 marches on held
  countries where there were 5, 169 countries taken by force where there were 2, 161
  standing armies lost where there were 2 - and it did not change who wins. Expect the
  next version to be about balance.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 14 GAMES OF 80. When the Arkwrights go first it collapses 13
  times in 20, and that seating carries most of the war.
- THE ORBITAL RULES FIRE RARELY IN COMPUTER GAMES. 39 Batteries stood at the end and 8
  were shot down; 6 bombards were fired and all six missed; 2 refuels were drawn at a
  partner's station; 1 interception was fought; NO ARMY WAS LANDED FROM A CARRIER in
  eighty games, and no colony ship was ever caught beside an escort. The computer's
  orbital fights are one or two hulls a side. These rules are there for YOU.
- PASSAGE IS NEVER STRUCK between computer sides: they never get warm enough with each
  other at the moment they offer. You can strike it with them.
- THE BLOCKADE IS STILL MOSTLY THE PROSPECTORS' (85 colony-turns starved where they go
  second), a little less than before now that Batteries stand.
- THE REPORT STILL SAYS "ARMY" in its march lines.
- SPACE EVENTS CAN STILL FIND NOTHING TO HIT: about three cards a game.
- TWO OF THE FOUR SPECIAL SPACE BUILDINGS ARE STILL NEVER BUILT BY THE COMPUTER (the
  Archivists' Heliostat and the Custodians' Academy). You can build either by hand.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION AND REFUEL EVER GET STRUCK between computer sides, and most bargains
  are later broken.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods. As last
  version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are dealt the leftovers.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- On an 800-pixel-tall window the Faction window is taller than the screen and opens at
  the very top; on a taller window it sits under the top bar.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
