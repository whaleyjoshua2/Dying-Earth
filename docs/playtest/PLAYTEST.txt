DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.07.2
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
   (Windows only again; there is no Linux zip for 0.07.2.)

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.07.1
-------------------------
This one is about the map of Earth and the words on it. Nothing about how the game is
won has changed, and no balance number moved.

- THE MAP FOLLOWS REAL BORDERS. Each region of Earth is now made of whole countries -
  India's region is India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka; Russia stops at Russia's
  border; Kazakhstan is no longer cut in half. An island belongs to its country. The
  lines you see are the lines the game plays by.
- TWO NEW REGIONS, fourteen in all: Japan (with Korea), cut out of China's region, and
  Saudi Arabia (the Arabian Peninsula), cut out of Iran's. Both got a share of their
  parent's people and industry, so nothing was added to the world, only divided.
- REGIONS ARE NAMED FOR THEIR MAIN COUNTRY, and that country's flag is on the card:
  China, India, The United States, Brazil, Russia, Australia, The European Union, Iran,
  Egypt, Nigeria, Indonesia, Mexico, Japan and Saudi Arabia. "East Asia" is now called
  China everywhere - map, cards, roster, reports.
- EVERY REGION HAS A COLOUR OF ITS OWN while nobody holds it. Once a side takes it, it
  wears that side's colour, a little stronger, with a light coastline. The region colours
  were checked against the four side colours so they cannot be mistaken for each other.
- YOU PICK YOUR START BY CLICKING THE GLOBE. The list of fourteen buttons is gone. Point
  at a region to read its card, click it to choose it, then press Begin. Every region's
  name is written on the globe, so you can find it.
- THE COUNTRY CARD IS TIDIER. Buttons that cost nothing no longer say "(free)". Prices
  are pictures: "Factory 20 [cart]" instead of "Factory (20 Materials)". Pointing at a
  build button now tells you how long it takes ("Ready next turn"). The Influence
  buttons are gone from the card: spend from the bottom-right corner instead.
- THE ROSTER ON THE RIGHT HAS PICTURES AND RINGS. Every row starts with a small picture
  saying what it is - a warship, a colony ship, a station, a colony, a region, or a
  shield for an army. A row that still wants an order from you ends in an open ring;
  the ring fills in once you have given one. The count-and-filter on each heading from
  0.07.1 is gone. The same pictures appear in front of names on the maps, on cards and
  in the Report.
- THE TOP BAR: Influence now stands before Research; the buttons are a bit bigger; and
  every button names its key: Tech Tree (T), Climate Panel (C), Victory (V), Trading (R),
  Save (Ctrl+S), the map swap (Tab), Back (Esc). END TURN IS ENTER. If you have Influence
  unspent, Enter asks first; Enter again confirms. While a report or a message is up,
  only Esc does anything.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > click a region on the globe, then Begin. The
  computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate button under the cards: the
  computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a region for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. Tab switches to the Solar System Map;
  Esc goes back.
- Build things with the buttons on a card. Hover a build button to see what it makes and
  how long it takes. Hover almost any number: if a rule sets it, the rule is there.
- Spend Influence on other regions to win them over. Your home region starts held.
  Click a region and use the Spend button in the bottom-right corner. Press Defence to
  spread what is left over the places you might lose; tick "every turn" to have it done
  for you each turn, as orders you can still cancel.
- Every region has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the region throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the region they stand in.
- Open the Climate Panel (C). The coloured bar marks every point where the world changes
  for good, including where Antarctica opens. The top bar shows this turn's net
  pollution beside the temperature.
- Getting off Earth: put a Shipyard on your station first - ships are only built at a
  Shipyard. Muster Emigrants in a region with a Launch Site, lift them, and load them on
  a Colony Ship. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only in a launch window; hover
  Mars to see when. Remember a colony's size limit: land people before you plan buildings.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- DO THE BORDERS READ? Could you find the region you wanted on the globe? Did any
  border surprise you - a country you expected somewhere else?
- COULD YOU TELL A HELD REGION FROM AN UNHELD ONE? Unheld regions wear their own colour,
  held ones wear a side's colour. Was that ever confusing, especially India and the
  United States, which are both green?
- DID THE START SCREEN WORK WITHOUT THE LIST? Did you miss it?
- THE ROSTER RINGS: did you understand the open ring without being told? Did the small
  pictures read at that size, or are any of them a smudge?
- DID ENTER ENDING THE TURN EVER SURPRISE YOU?
- DO THE NEW REGIONS FEEL RIGHT? Japan and Saudi Arabia are new and their numbers are
  a first guess.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE CUSTODIANS ARE TOO STRONG. Over eighty computer-only games on the new map they win
  70, from any seat. That is the next thing to fix and this version deliberately did
  not touch it.
- THE ARCHIVISTS ALMOST NEVER WIN in those eighty, though the Archive itself gets built.
  Getting twelve people to live at it is the part that fails.
- Every side's victory target was set against a computer that never competed for it, so
  the numbers are all suspect.
- RESEARCH IS NOT A CONSTRAINT. The whole tech tree gets finished in 73 of 80 games.
  Whether it should is a question for a balance version.
- DEFENCE ONLY REACTS AT THE LAST MOMENT. It answers a rival who could take a place next
  turn, and nobody sooner. Known, and not yet decided what to do about it.
- SAUDI ARABIA EARNS NO DUCATS: its economy figure is too small for the formula to pay
  anything. A first-guess number; tell us if it feels wrong.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. Older than this version.
- No sound, no tutorial beyond the first Report.
