DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.09.1
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.09.0 and before will not load in this version: ships can now carry a
   warhead and worlds remember who settled them first, so an old file describes a board
   this version cannot read. The Load screen says so.
7. This version ships for Windows and for Linux. On Linux, read RUN-ON-LINUX.txt in the
   folder instead of steps 2 to 5.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.09.0
-------------------------
Most of this version is the game answering for itself after its first real playtest:
saying what it knows, and doing what it said. Every rule below the computer plays too.

NEW RULES
- NUKES. A new ship, the MISSILE CARRIER, carries one warhead. It cannot fight, and it
  needs a new Tech, Missile Technology. Fired at a country, colony or station a rival
  holds, from an orbit you hold outright, it guts the place: most buildings burn, about
  half the people die, and a country loses its standing army and a level of industry. It
  can be fired over Earth. On Earth it fouls the air AND raises the natural sink a little
  for good. A carrier reloads only at a shipyard of your own.
- THE SINK KEEPS WHAT IT IS GIVEN. "The Sink Weakens" now takes 2.0 off the sink instead
  of setting it to 4.0, so anything that raised the sink is not wiped out by it.
- BEING FIRST TO A WORLD PAYS. The first side ever to found a ground colony on a world off
  Earth gets a one-off windfall of Influence (5 at the Moon, 15 at Mars, 20 at Phobos or
  Deimos) and +1 Influence a turn while it holds that colony. Stations and Antarctica do
  not count; Venus has no ground to claim.
- BATTLES IN SPACE COST FUEL: 2 from every ship in the fight, on both sides. A ship that
  cannot pay fights at half strength until it refuels, and a warship low on fuel cannot
  hold an orbit, blockade or intercept.
- A LAUNCH SITE REACHES ANY ORBIT OF EARTH. You can lift pioneers and armies straight onto
  a ship at your station's ring; you no longer have to bring it down to low orbit first.
- A BATTERY FIRES ON A SHIP BLOCKADING ITS ORBIT, so a defended station under blockade is
  a battle, not just a blockade that does nothing.
- A SHUT BARRACKS raises and repairs no army. A SHUT HABITAT still houses its people, but
  while people are living in it the whole colony makes everything but Energy at half.
- THE ARCHIVISTS' CONDITION: 125 Research into the Archive, the old cap on it gone. The
  Archive may now be ordered before The Upload is researched; The Upload is still needed
  to win.

THE GAME TELLS YOU WHAT IT KNOWS
- A place you hold is PRESSED when a rival is within 10 of your Standing there. Pressed
  places are listed under the Influence slider; click one to go there.
- Your first Report names your own Victory Condition, with its real figures.
- The Energy figure on the top bar TURNS RED when your next Income will shut buildings
  down, and its hover says which, in order.
- Pointing at a building's figure SHOWS THE ARITHMETIC: "2 base x 1.32 for 1.45B people x
  1.10 for Education x 1.25 as the Custodians = 3.63, rounded down to 3". Education counts
  twice in a Research Lab, and now it says so.
- A button that is shut because your ship is in the wrong orbit SAYS WHAT TO DO: "Move
  this Ship to Earth, at ISS, then refuel next turn."
- On a country's card, the PIONEERS block (Recruit, and the buttons that send them) now
  sits above the building tiles, where it can be seen.
- The Report's "have not forgiven" lines are gone. It now tells you only when a side falls
  to a worse mood toward you, or you toward them: "The Prospectors are now Cold toward
  you."

SEVEN THINGS THAT WERE UNTRUE, NOW TRUE
- A build refusal says whether a shipyard is missing, still building or shut. A founding
  names the right site. A partial unload and a lift that ran out of room say who stayed.
  A lift fills as far as the room goes instead of refusing. The Research Directive line
  says what it bought. A quiet turn's Report opens with a headline. Relay Networks and the
  Arkwrights' Chorus now actually pay the Influence they always showed.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- NUKES: did you build one? Did being nuked by a computer side feel fair, or out of
  nowhere? Did you know how to stop one?
- PRESSED: did you notice the list? Did it make you spend Influence to hold a place?
- THE ARITHMETIC HOVERS: did you read them? Were they too long?
- THE RED ENERGY FIGURE: did it warn you in time?
- BEING FIRST TO A WORLD: did the windfall change where you sent your first colony?
- Anything that still says the same thing twice.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. These are known. You do not need to report them.

WHAT THIS VERSION KNOWS ABOUT ITSELF

- THE WIN COLUMN: Custodians 6, Prospectors 28, Arkwrights 3, Archivists 2, where last
  version was 5 / 31 / 1 / 0. The two sides that won nothing now win something; the
  Prospectors still take over a third of all games.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 41 GAMES OF 80, where it was 42.
- THE WAR IN SPACE HAS STARTED. The computer sides now blockade in 20 games of 80 and
  fire nukes in 21 (39 launches in all). Battles in orbit are still rare: 7 games of 80.
- THE ARCHIVISTS RARELY WIN ON THEIR CONDITION. The Archive now gets built in 12 games of
  80 and finished in 8, but it finishes around turn 33 of 36, too late to upload the 12
  colonists their win asks for.
- NOBODY CAN ACCEPT OR DECLINE AN ACCORD OFFERED TO THEM - INCLUDING YOU. The game still
  answers on your behalf.
- THE THREAT LINE ON A COUNTRY'S CARD STILL QUOTES THE FIRST EXCHANGE, while every other
  odds figure is the whole battle's. Deliberate, until both move together.

CARRIED OVER, STILL TRUE

- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
