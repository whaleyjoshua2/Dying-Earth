DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.09.0
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.08.8 and before will not load in this version: what a building costs,
   what a unit of population means and where a ship sits all changed under them, so an
   old file describes a board this version cannot read. The Load screen says so. This
   version ships for Windows only.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.8
-------------------------
The economy. Last version was about fighting; this one is about paying for things. Every
rule below the computer plays too.

- EVERYTHING YOU BUILD NOW COSTS WORK AS WELL AS ORE. The ore is Materials, as before,
  paid in full when you give the order. The work is a new thing called WIDGETS, drawn as
  a cog. A place makes so many Widgets a turn and pours them into whatever it is
  building; a building is finished when enough work has gone into it, instead of after a
  fixed number of turns. Widgets are not saved up, not traded and never go in your
  stockpile: whatever a place does not use this turn is gone.
- SO MINES AND FACTORIES DO DIFFERENT JOBS NOW. A Mine digs the ore. A Factory does the
  work. On Earth the Mine is a new building; every country that started with a Factory
  now starts with a Mine beside it, so you dig exactly what you always did and the work
  is new on top. Off Earth the Factory is a new building for a colony or a station, where
  before a colony's core did all the work itself.
- A PLACE BUILDS SOMETHING EVEN WITH NO FACTORY. A country makes four Widgets a turn on
  its own, and one more for every level of industry it has built up; a colony or station
  makes four from its core. So a bare country still puts up a one-turn building in a
  turn, as it always did, and a built-up one goes a little faster.
- YOU MAY ORDER AS MANY BUILDS AS YOU LIKE AT A PLACE, and they are worked on IN THE
  ORDER YOU GAVE THEM: the turn's work fills the first one and flows on to the next.
  Nothing is split. Every build button, every tile and the top bar's cog say where a
  build has got to - "3 of 8" - and how long it should take at that place's rate. Paying
  Ducats outright still jumps the queue.
- ONE POPULATION POINT IS NOW ONE MILLION PEOPLE, where it was five million. Every
  country's figure went up fivefold to match and nothing else about the world changed,
  so China reads 1454.4 (1.45B) where it read 290.9. One colonist is still one point -
  now a million people instead of five million.
- ARMIES ARE RAISED FROM PEOPLE. Raising an army in a country takes a million of its
  people, on top of its Materials and its Widgets, and is refused if the country has not
  got them. At a colony it takes one colonist, and is refused if only one lives there.
  The people do not come back: an army that dies takes them with it. A country's own
  standing army is the state's and costs you nobody.
- SHIPS GO TO A PARTICULAR ORBIT. Every world now has several named orbits: LOW ORBIT,
  hugging the ground, and one for each station slot. You choose which one when you send a
  ship, and the Transits list on the ship card has a line for every one. Moving between
  orbits once you are there costs 1 fuel from the ship's own tank.
  - LOW ORBIT IS THE LANE TO THE GROUND: armies land from it, colonists unload into a
    ground colony from it, a new colony is founded from it, and a lift from a launch site
    arrives in it. Orbital control is held there and nowhere else.
  - A STATION IS ONLY REACHED FROM ITS OWN ORBIT: to unload into it, refuel at it,
    attack it or blockade it, your ship has to be sitting in that orbit.
  - SO YOU CAN BLOCKADE A STATION AT LAST. In every version before this one the order
    needed a warship already parked in the station's slot and no button ever sent one
    there, so no human player could ever give it.
  - A BATTERY NOW COVERS ONE ORBIT, not the whole sky. On a ground colony it holds low
    orbit, which is what keeps a fleet off your surface. On a station it shields that
    station. This is narrower than last version gave it, on purpose.
  - A BATTLE IS FOUGHT INSIDE ONE ORBIT, so two fights over one world are two battles,
    two records and two marks on the map.
- TAKING A COLONY OR A STATION WITH INFLUENCE COSTS TWICE AS MUCH. A colonist is worth 20
  where it was 10, and there is now a flat 40 under every place off Earth, so an empty
  colony is no longer nearly free. A station and a ground colony with the same crew now
  cost the same, which they never did before. Taking countries on Earth is unchanged, and
  so is the extra you must beat a current holder by.
- NEARLY HALF THE EVENT CARDS NOW ASK YOU A QUESTION. The deck is still forty cards, but
  every duplicate has been cut and eighteen new cards put in their places, so no two
  cards in the deck are the same. Eighteen of them offer you a bargain with a cost on
  each side - pay the relief, or go without and take the unrest. A card like that is dealt
  AT THE START OF THE TURN, before you give any orders, so you can play the turn knowing
  the answer, and THE TURN WILL NOT END UNTIL YOU HAVE ANSWERED. Every side at the table
  is asked the same card in the same turn, and the Report tells you what they each said.
  If you cannot afford the offer you are asked anyway, with that button dead. The other
  twenty-two cards are the old kind and still simply happen after orders.
- THE GAME ENDS ON A PAGE THAT SAYS HOW IT WENT. A "Chronicle" button on the game-over box
  opens it: the four sides RANKED rather than listed in seat order, with the tiebreak
  named where one decided it and a sentence each saying how they ended and what held them
  back; a table of their final materials, fuel, energy, Ducats, colonists off Earth,
  countries, colonies, research and blame; and the population and temperature charts.
- THE ODDS YOU ARE SHOWN BEFORE A FIGHT ARE NOW THE WHOLE BATTLE'S. This is the biggest
  fix in the version. Every odds figure in the game - on an attack button's hover, on a
  ship stack's card, in the battle record afterwards - used to be the chance of winning
  THE FIRST EXCHANGE, which is not what anybody wants to know. It was wrong in both
  directions: on one board it read 19.8% where the real chance of winning was 1%, and on
  another 61% where it was 86%. It now says "the chance of holding the field when the
  battle is over", and it means it.
- A REFUSAL NOW TELLS YOU THE RULE, NOT THE PRICE. Ordering something that is not allowed
  used to answer with what it would have cost. It answers with why you may not.
- THE REPORT NAMES YOUR ARMIES. Armies have had names for four versions and the march
  lines never used them.
- A RELAY OR AN EMBASSY IS AN EYE. One working Embassy in a country you hold lets you read
  every rival's income on Earth building by building, on their own card; one working Relay
  at a colony or station does the same for everything at that world. No eye, no figures.
- SMALLER THINGS: the top bar reads "20 / 20" instead of "20 of 20"; the temperature reads
  "+1.7 C" with the heading-to figure moved onto its hover; the End Turn sun has room on
  its left; and the four yields on a "found a colony" button sit beside the words instead
  of under them.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- EVERY BUILD COSTS MATERIALS AND WIDGETS. The Materials leave your stockpile at once; the
  Widgets are the work the place puts in, four a turn for every old build turn. The card
  shows the place's rate and the queue; the tile shows "3 of 8"; the top bar's cog shows
  what all your places make and what they used.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Set the amount on the slider at the bottom right
  and press Spend, or Max. Off Earth it now costs twice what it did.
- ARMIES are raised from a region's card, and cost a million of that region's people as
  well as Materials and Widgets. They march to a neighbouring region from the Armies list
  on that card. Arriving in a region you do not hold is an attack; hover the button first
  - the percentage is your chance of holding the field when the whole battle is over.
  A region's own standing army can march too, but away from home it loses its people's
  defence.
- WARSHIPS are built at a shipyard and fight in orbit. Send one to a NAMED ORBIT: low
  orbit for the ground, a station's own orbit to touch that station. A warship sitting in
  a rival station's orbit can be ordered to Blockade.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  what they have under way, what bargains stand between them, where you smear them, and
  where you request carbon credits. Your own page is where you greenwash.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider (and the Prospectors' Fund slider).
- The TRADING window (R) buys and sells goods.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - the sun at the bottom right, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the
  temperature in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- WIDGETS: did you work out what the cog meant without being told? Did you ever wonder
  why a building was taking so long? Did you notice work being wasted at a place with
  nothing to build?
- THE BUILD QUEUE: did you expect the first thing you ordered to be finished first? Did
  you want to reorder it?
- THE CARDS THAT ASK: was the question clear? Did you feel the two sides were a real
  choice, or was one obviously right every time? Was being unable to end the turn
  annoying or fair?
- ORBITS: did you know which orbit to send a ship to? Did you find the blockade? Was the
  Transits list, with a line for every orbit, too long?
- THE ATTACK ODDS: now that the number is the whole battle, did it match what happened?
- THE CHRONICLE at the end: did you read it? Was anything you wanted missing?
- ARMIES COSTING PEOPLE: did you feel it? Did a refusal ever surprise you?
- Anything that still says the same thing twice.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. These are known. You do not need to report them.

WHAT THIS VERSION KNOWS ABOUT ITSELF

- THE WIN COLUMN MOVED HARD, AND WE KNOW WHY. Custodians 5, Prospectors 31, Arkwrights 1,
  Archivists 0, where last version was 43 / 22 / 1 / 0. THE CUSTODIANS ARE WINNING FAR
  LESS THAN THEY WERE. It is not the pace of building: it is Earth's emissions. Pulling
  the Mine out of the Factory means two buildings pollute on Earth where one did, and the
  games used to end a hair under the collapse line. The next version is about balance and
  this is its first lever.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 42 GAMES OF 80, where it was 14, for the same reason.
- THE WAR IN SPACE IS QUIET. In eighty games the computer sides ordered TWO blockades and
  opened effectively no battles in orbit at all. A side keeps about one warship and the
  new rule rightly parks it in low orbit, where no station sits, so it is never next to
  anything to shut. No army was landed from a carrier, no interception was fought and no
  bombard was fired. These rules are there for YOU.
- THE THREAT LINE ON A COUNTRY'S CARD STILL QUOTES THE FIRST EXCHANGE, while every other
  odds figure in the game is now the whole battle's. This was left alone deliberately:
  that figure is the one the computer's own attack decision compares against, and moving
  one without the other would make the amber warning a lie. It will move when both do.
- THE SHIP STACK CARD'S "FOUND A COLONY" BUTTONS ARE RAGGED down their right edge, now
  that the four yields sit beside the words instead of under them. Cosmetic, and known.
- NOBODY CAN ACCEPT OR DECLINE AN ACCORD OFFERED TO THEM - INCLUDING YOU. When a side
  offers you a bargain the game answers on your behalf by the same rule it uses for the
  computer sides. There has never been a button. So diplomacy is one-sided in a way no
  rule ever decided, and it wants a ticket of its own.
- AN ARMED WARSHIP AGAINST THREE UNARMED HULLS READS ABOUT 24%, which looks wrong and is
  not: holding an orbit means clearing every hull out of it, which is not the same thing
  as taking orbital control.

CARRIED OVER, STILL TRUE

- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION BARGAINS STAND between computer sides at the end of a game. Passage
  is still never struck between them - they never get warm enough with each other at the
  moment they offer - and no refuel was ever drawn at a partner's station in eighty
  games. You can strike either with them.
- CARDS CAN STILL FIND NOTHING TO HIT: about two a game now, roughly half what it was,
  because with no duplicate copies a card spent off Earth cannot come round again.
- THE COMPUTER ALMOST NEVER BUILDS THE ARCHIVISTS' HELIOSTAT OR THE CUSTODIANS' ACADEMY
  (none and one, in eighty games). You can build either by hand.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods. As
  last version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are dealt the leftovers.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- On an 800-pixel-tall window the Faction window is taller than the screen and opens at
  the very top; on a taller window it sits under the top bar.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
