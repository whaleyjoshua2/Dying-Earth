DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.1.0.6
=============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.1.0.5 and before do not load: two Nation States joined the board, and the
   Load screen says so.
7. This version ships for Windows and for Linux. On Linux, read RUN-ON-LINUX.txt in the
   folder instead of steps 2 to 5.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.1.0.5
--------------------------
The fission version: a new resource, and people who move on their own.

FISSIONABLES
- A SEVENTH RESOURCE, found off Earth only: a Mine makes them beside its Materials, by its
  site's figure (the Moon has two or three sites with any; Mars, its moons, Mercury, Ceres
  and Vesta are richer). Bought, sold and traded like Fuel.
- NUCLEAR HULLS: once Nuclear Rockets is researched a Ship may be built nuclear, with a
  second tank of Fissionables (a tenth of its Fuel tank) filled at the build. It alone gets
  the Tech's shorter crossings, and each leg burns a tenth of its Fuel in Fissionables too.
  A chemical hull can be REFITTED nuclear at a Shipyard in its orbit (Refit nuclear on the
  Ship card). A Warhead costs half a Fissionable.
- THE REACTOR: a new power plant for everyone, on Earth and as a Module off it, behind Clean
  Power: 8 Energy a turn for 0.1 Fissionables and 2 Ducats, no emissions. The Archivists'
  own building, which was called the Reactor, is the ACCUMULATOR now; its rule is unchanged.

PEOPLE WHO MOVE ON THEIR OWN
- FERRY ROUTES: a Colony Ship can be set to ferry between where it stands and a Colony or
  station of yours: it loads, flies, unloads and comes back, refuelling at each depot and
  waiting for windows. The card says the round trip's Fuel.
- THE REFUGEE CONVOY has a third answer, "Send them to the stars": six refugees become
  Pioneers and lift to your Ships and stations in orbit with room; the rest are lost.
- THE FIRST CHILD BORN OFF EARTH is a Moment and +5 Influence, once a game.
- AN EMPTY COLONY SHIP over a Colony of yours can be SCRAPPED into a free Habitat there -
  the way out for a stranded one.

WAR OVER COLONIES
- A COLONY CARD names its THREATS: rival warships in orbit, Armies aboard Carriers, Ships on
  their way, and a rival's Standing at half the price.
- A NEW ARMY joins the stance of your Armies at its place (dug in at once, if they are).
- AN INHERITED ARMY stays on Hold until you order it: it no longer attacks on its own.

THE FIGURES AND THE BOARD
- THE ARKWRIGHTS: transit x0.8, Habitats and Colony Ships x1.25 (5, 7 and 10 aboard),
  stations x0.75, 50 Fuel to start. THE ARCHIVISTS start with 25 Ducats. THE PROSPECTORS'
  bar is 6,500.
- TWO MORE NATION STATES: CANADA (with Greenland) and ETHIOPIA (the Horn), 22 in all.
- THE CLIMATE was re-swept with them: the world heats faster, and about a quarter of
  computer games now end in Collapse.

THE WINDOW
- The eight CAPITAL BUILDINGS have pictures.
- Cards and bars are a touch lighter; the time of day sits beside End Turn; a Standing
  chip is a pill.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE CONTEST: did a computer Faction outbid you on a Region you were climbing? Did you
  see it coming, and what did you do about it?
- THE DEFENCE: did you take a computer's home Region, and how many turns did it take?
- THE PROSPECTORS' FUND: at 6,250, did you reach it, and on which turn?
- THE HOVERS: was any one-line hover now too short to tell you the rule?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.
