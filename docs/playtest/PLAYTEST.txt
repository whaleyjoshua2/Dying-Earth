DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.3
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. ON UBUNTU OR ANOTHER LINUX: take the "-linux" zip instead. There is no .exe in it;
   open a terminal in the unzipped folder and run   ./dying-earth   . The file
   RUN-ON-LINUX.txt beside it says what to do if the window does not open, and where
   the saves go. It is built on Ubuntu 22.04 and wants 22.04 or newer.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.2
-------------------------
Last version was about what the other three sides think of you. This one is about the
four sides being DIFFERENT from each other. Each now has something only it can do, and
two new rules charge everybody for choices they used to get free.

- YOUR RESEARCH NO LONGER HAS TO GO INTO THE TECH TREE. On the Victory panel there is now
  a slider: RESEARCH DIRECTIVE. Whatever share you keep back goes somewhere useful to your
  side alone - the Custodians push it into the carbon sink, permanently; the Prospectors
  turn it into money; the Arkwrights into fuel; the Archivists into their Archive, as
  before. Everyone may keep back up to half. The Archivists may keep back all of it, and
  the bottom half of their slider is greyed out because they can never go below half.
- BUT KEEPING IT BACK COSTS YOU FRIENDS. Contribute less than 85% of your research to the
  shared tech tree and every other side thinks slightly worse of you, that turn. Give all
  of it and they think slightly better. It is read fresh each turn, so a side that starts
  contributing again is forgiven at once, and it never leaves a permanent scar.
- EVERY SIDE NOW HAS A SPECIAL BUILDING IN SPACE, not just on Earth. The Archivists get
  the HELIOSTAT (a solar array that makes one more power, anywhere). The Prospectors get
  the EXCHANGE (a trade post that pays one more coin). The Arkwrights get the CHORUS (a
  relay that gives you one more influence for every six people living at that colony).
  The Custodians keep their Academy. Each costs the same as the ordinary building it
  replaces, and each survives being captured.
- THE ARKWRIGHTS CAN SOUND AN EXODUS CALL. On a country they hold, 50 coins buys two turns
  of double recruiting - sixteen pioneers a turn instead of eight - and while it runs those
  people cost the country the ORDINARY amount rather than the Arkwrights' usual double.
  Once per country, ever.
- THE PROSPECTORS NOW HOARD MONEY, NOT ORE. Their Venture Capital Fund holds coins, filled
  by a share of your income each turn before you can spend any of it, and you win at 2000.
  The slider goes up to 80%, and that is a real choice now: coins are what buys influence,
  pays relief and mends ships, so banking a lot genuinely hurts.
- YOU MUST HOLD A COUNTRY THREE TURNS BEFORE YOU CAN REMAKE IT. The Strip Permit, the
  Leapfrog and the Exodus Call all now need the country to have been yours for three whole
  turns. The turn you take it does not count. Hover the greyed button and it tells you the
  turn it opens.
- TWO NEW TECHS. BENEFICIATION (extraction) makes every mine produce a tenth more, wherever
  it stands. RELAY NETWORKS (off-world living) makes a relay worth two influence instead of
  one.
- THE TECH TREE COSTS MORE and now SCROLLS. Coastal Engineering is 15, everything on the
  second rung is 32 and everything on the third is 48. The tree is also drawn a tenth
  smaller and scrolls if your screen is short, because it had grown taller than a 1280x800
  window and two victory techs were falling off the bottom.
- WORDS. "Emigrants" are now PIONEERS and "muster" is now RECRUIT. The Arkwrights' rule
  called "Steerage" is now COACH CLASS. Nothing about how any of it works has changed.
- TWO LONG EXPLANATIONS IN THE FACTION WINDOW ARE NOW MOUSE-OVERS, so the page is about a
  fifth shorter. Hover the "Blame" and "Relations" headings.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Spend it from the bar at the bottom right.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  and what bargains stand between them.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE RESEARCH DIRECTIVE SLIDER: did you find it? Did you understand what keeping research
  back would GET you, before you moved it? Did the warning about losing friends change
  what you did, or did you ignore it?
- THE 85% LINE: did you ever deliberately cross it, or did you just leave the slider alone?
  If you left it alone, say so - that would mean the choice is not worth taking.
- YOUR SIDE'S SPECIAL BUILDING IN SPACE: did you ever build it? Did you know you had one?
- THE THREE-TURN WAIT: did you try to strip or leapfrog a country you had just taken, and
  get refused? Did the message tell you enough?
- THE EXODUS CALL, if you played the Arkwrights: could you afford it? Fifty coins is a lot
  in a poor country, and we suspect it is out of reach where you usually start.
- THE MONEY HOARD, if you played the Prospectors: what share did you bank, and did banking
  it leave you unable to do things you wanted to do?
- THE TECH TREE'S NEW SHAPE: can you still see the whole thing? Did you have to scroll, and
  did you notice you could?
- THE NEW WORDS: does "Pioneer" read right? Does "Coach Class"?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first.

- THE PROSPECTORS NOW WIN TOO MUCH, AND THE ARCHIVISTS ALMOST NEVER DO. Out of eighty
  games: Prospectors 30, Custodians 26, Arkwrights 8, Archivists 1. Last version it was
  Custodians 37, Prospectors 10, Archivists 7, Arkwrights 3. So the side that was running
  away has come down and the weakest side has nearly tripled, which is what this version
  was for - but the Prospectors have taken over as the runaway and the Archivists have
  fallen off a cliff. We know, and we know why (next item). It is the first job of the
  next version.
- WHY THE ARCHIVISTS COLLAPSED, since it is not a mystery. Their way to win is to pour
  research into their Archive - which means they are almost always keeping research back,
  which means the new 85% rule charges them every single turn of every single game. On top
  of that their special space building is one they never want to build, and their victory
  tech moved further away. Three separate changes landed on one side and nobody was
  watching the total. If you play them and it feels hopeless, that is the reason.
- TWO OF THE FOUR SPECIAL SPACE BUILDINGS ARE NEVER BUILT BY THE COMPUTER. The Archivists'
  Heliostat and the Custodians' Academy both stand at zero across all eighty games; the
  Prospectors' Exchange and the Arkwrights' Chorus work fine. The Heliostat's reason is
  known - the Archivists' reactor means they are never short of power, so they never want a
  solar array. The Academy's reason is not known and is older than this version. You can
  build either by hand.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES LESS OFTEN THAN IT DID - 15 games in 80, against 23 last version.
  That is the one number moving in a direction nobody aimed at. Tell us whether the climate
  still feels like a threat or whether you stopped watching the temperature.
- THE EXODUS CALL ALMOST NEVER FIRES unless the Arkwrights happen to start somewhere rich.
  In eighty games it was sounded 177 times in the twenty where they went first, and ZERO
  times in the other sixty. Fifty coins is simply more than a poor country makes. Say
  whether you could ever afford it.
- NEARLY HALF OF ALL PAIRS END A GAME SCARRED, as last version: 41% to 48% of the twelve
  relationships carry a permanent floor by the end, and a typical pair ends three to five
  points below neutral. The scale still says something, but if everyone ends up hating
  everyone it stops meaning much. Say if it felt that way.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION EVER GETS STRUCK. The computer sides offer and accept that one and
  nothing else, because passage and refuelling are worth almost nothing on today's board.
  Both are built for when the game reaches Mars. Roughly three quarters of the bargains
  struck in a game are later broken.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides only ever buy materials
  and NEVER sell anything. So those two prices move only when YOU trade them. Giving the
  computer an appetite to sell is a later job.
- THE TECH TREE STILL FINISHES, and now FASTER - around turn 26, where last version it was
  turn 31 to 36 - even though everything in it costs more. The extra research from the new
  directive and from research agreements more than pays for the price rise.
- THE PICTURES ON THE NEW SPACE BUILDINGS ARE FIRST PICKS from a free icon set. The
  Arkwrights' Chorus in particular is a satellite with signal lines that goes muddy at the
  small size a build tile draws it. Say which ones misled you.
- THE SPACEPORT AND THE LAUNCH SITE look alike at the small size a build box draws them
  at: both are a tall thing over a low thing. Say so if you ever mistook one for the other.
- A SPECIAL BUILDING SITS AT THE BOTTOM of a colony's build list rather than in the place
  of the ordinary building it replaces. Cosmetic.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out. Older than this version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are always dealt the leftovers.
  This is being looked at and may explain some of the imbalance above.
- No sound.
