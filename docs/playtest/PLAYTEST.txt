DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.7
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.08.6 load in this version: no rule changed. Saves from 0.08.5 and before
   will not; the Load screen says so. This version ships for Windows only.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.6
-------------------------
Nothing in the rules. This version is about what you can SEE. Last version rewrote the
army; this one lets you read the war.

- THE ATTACK BUTTON TELLS YOU WHO YOU ARE ATTACKING. It used to read "attack Russia
  (61%)" and nothing else. Now it reads "attack Russia", and hovering it says who
  defends, at what strength, what they defend at (dug in or not), how hurt they are,
  and what the percentage is a chance OF: winning the first exchange, your strength
  against theirs. Landing an army at a rival's colony gets the same hover.
- A THREAT LINE ON YOUR COUNTRIES. Under the line that names the rival closest to
  taking a country by influence, a second line names the rival army next door most
  likely to take it by force: "The Prospectors' 2nd Russian Army, strength 3, stands
  next door in Russia." It turns amber when their odds reach the point where the
  computer would attack. It never tells you their orders - only that they are there.
- LAST TURN'S BATTLE IS ON THE MAP. The turn after a battle, a ring in the attacker's
  colour circles the country (or colony) where it was fought; hover it for who attacked
  and what happened, click its top edge for the Report. Every army shield there is
  outlined in that colour, and any shield whose army is hurt wears a red dot. Battles
  in orbit are not marked.
- ARMY ORDERS ARE WHERE THE ARMIES ARE. The stance row and the march buttons now sit in
  the Armies list on the country card, under each army, instead of at the very bottom of
  the card where you had to scroll for them. The whole block is a little larger.
- STANCES EXPLAIN THEMSELVES. Hover Attack, Hold, Dig In, Evade, Intercept or Blockade
  on the stance row and it says what it does. Intercept, for warships, "fights what
  arrives this turn, before it can land" - nobody could find that out before. Ships in
  the roster now show their stance in brackets, as armies do.
- OFFLINE BUILDINGS LOOK OFFLINE. A building shut for lack of energy, knocked out by an
  event, in a colony whose grid is down, or an Archive under occupation is dimmed with
  the word "offline" in its corner; hovering says why. Before, it looked exactly like a
  working one.
- END TURN HAS ITS OWN COLUMN, at the right of the command cluster, at the bottom.
- LESS SAYING THINGS TWICE: eight places where the screen repeated itself are gone. The
  "(hover a button...)" hints on headings, the sentence under the climate panel's blame
  block, the victory text repeated over its own bars, and so on.
- THE PROSPECTORS' FUND IS OFF THE TOP BAR. It is on the Victory panel, where its slider
  is. No other side's fund was ever on the bar.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Set the amount on the slider at the bottom right
  and press Spend, or Max.
- ARMIES are raised from a region's card and march to a neighbouring region from the
  Armies list on that card. Arriving in a region you do not hold is an attack; hover the
  button first. A region's own standing army can march too, but away from home it
  loses its people's defence. Warships are built at a
  shipyard and fight in orbit; a warship in a rival station's slot can be ordered to
  Blockade.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  what they have under way, what bargains stand between them, where you smear them, and
  where you request carbon credits. Your own page is where you greenwash.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider (and the Prospectors' Fund slider).
- The TRADING window (R) buys and sells goods.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - the sun at the bottom right, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the
  temperature in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE ATTACK HOVER: before you attacked, did you know who you were fighting and what the
  number meant? Was six lines too many?
- THE THREAT LINE: did it warn you in time? Did you want to know the army's orders, or
  was "it is there" enough?
- THE BATTLE RING: did you notice it? Did you find the Report from it? Did you miss a
  mark for battles in orbit?
- ARMY ORDERS IN THE ARMIES LIST: better than the bottom of the card? Is the block the
  right size?
- STANCE HOVERS: did you hover them? Did you use Intercept once you knew what it did?
- OFFLINE BUILDINGS: did you notice one go dim? Was the cause on the hover enough?
- THE SUN in its own column: still obvious?
- Anything that still says the same thing twice.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. They are the same eighty games as last version, move for move, because no rule
changed.

- THE WIN COLUMN IS WHERE 0.08.6 LEFT IT. Out of eighty games: Custodians 43, Prospectors
  21, Arkwrights 2, Archivists 0. Last version's rules hardened the defender and the
  Prospectors were the side that attacked; this version changed nothing about that.
  Expect the next version to be about balance.
- THE COMPUTER NO LONGER WINS ON THE GROUND. It marches one army at a time and only at
  60% odds, and a single raised army clears that against almost nobody. Two armies
  together would; it never masses. This is measured behaviour, not a rule: YOU can mass.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 14 GAMES OF 80. When the Arkwrights go first it collapses 12
  times in 20, and that seating ends at +3.0 C. The other three end cooler.
- THREE RULES NEVER FIRE IN EIGHTY COMPUTER GAMES: no occupation is abandoned, no army is
  landed at a colony, and no neutral country is ever threatened (every country is taken
  by influence before the first army exists). They will matter to YOU if you play that
  way.
- BATTLES IN ORBIT ARE NOT MARKED ON THE MAP, and in computer games that is most of them
  (57 of 62). The Report still lists every one.
- THE BLOCKADE IS STILL MOSTLY THE PROSPECTORS' (107 colony-turns starved where they go
  second).
- THE REPORT STILL SAYS "ARMY" in its march lines.
- SPACE EVENTS CAN STILL FIND NOTHING TO HIT: about three cards a game.
- TWO OF THE FOUR SPECIAL SPACE BUILDINGS ARE STILL NEVER BUILT BY THE COMPUTER (the
  Archivists' Heliostat and the Custodians' Academy). You can build either by hand.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION EVER GETS STRUCK between computer sides, and most bargains are
  later broken. As last version.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods. As last
  version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are dealt the leftovers.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- On an 800-pixel-tall window the Faction window is taller than the screen and opens at
  the very top; on a taller window it sits under the top bar.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
