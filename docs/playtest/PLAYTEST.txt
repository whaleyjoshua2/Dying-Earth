DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.4
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. ON UBUNTU OR ANOTHER LINUX: take the "-linux" zip instead. There is no .exe in it;
   open a terminal in the unzipped folder and run   ./dying-earth   . The file
   RUN-ON-LINUX.txt beside it says what to do if the window does not open, and where
   the saves go. It is built on Ubuntu 22.04 and wants 22.04 or newer.
7. Saves from 0.08.3 will not load in this version; the Load screen says so.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.3
-------------------------
Last version made the four sides different from each other. This one is about what the
sides can DO to each other, and what they can SEE of each other.

- BLAME IS NOW A LEDGER, NOT A MEASUREMENT. Your blame is still the carbon you put in
  the air less the carbon you took out - but now other sides can write on it. Hover the
  "Blame" heading on the Faction window and it tells you what you are answerable for and
  where every part of it came from.
- SMEAR A RIVAL. On any rival's page in the Faction window (F) you can spend influence to
  lay blame on them: one influence puts two ppm on their ledger, for good. They are told
  who did it and think worse of you. It is a lie, which is the point.
- BUY CARBON CREDITS FROM THE CUSTODIANS. A fourth line in the Trading window (M). Each
  ppm you buy comes off your blame and costs about a coin, cheaper if the Custodians like
  you and dearer if they do not; if they hate you they will not sell at all. Ten a turn at
  most. Buying is an act of friendship both ways. If you ARE the Custodians you set the
  offer there - and you may sell more credit than you actually hold, in which case the
  difference lands on your own ledger as blame.
- AGITATE. On a country a rival holds, 15 coins and 5 influence raise its unrest by one
  point. Once a turn per country. The holder is told who did it. Note that unrest also
  falls by 1.5 a turn on its own, so one agitate a turn on a calm country does nothing
  lasting - it works on a country that is already restless, or when several sides lean on
  it together.
- BLAME NOW CHANGES HOW FAST YOUR INFLUENCE FADES. On countries you do NOT hold, a side
  answerable for less than an eighth of the world's blame loses 1 standing a turn instead
  of 2; a side answerable for half or more loses 3. Countries you hold still lose 1.
- SEA WALLS ARE NEVER DESTROYED. A wall now holds every sea-level rise, forever - but each
  rise it has held costs half a material a turn to keep, on top of its power. Miss the
  materials and it stands idle that turn. A storm surge no longer breaks a walled country;
  instead the coastal buildings there make 30% less next turn.
- THE PROSPECTORS' FUND. The share you bank is now a proper slider in single percents (the
  top fifth is greyed because 80% is the cap), the draw is called WITHDRAW and takes an
  amount, and the winning total is 2500 coins, up from 2000. The computer Prospectors play
  to 2500 too.
- YOU ARE WARNED WHEN A RIVAL IS CLOSE TO WINNING. A new interruption fires when a rival
  reaches three quarters of the way, and again when they have completed one half of their
  victory condition outright. It names the part they still need.
- EVERY PLACE YOU HOLD NAMES ITS CHALLENGER. The card of a country, colony or station you
  hold now says which rival is nearest to taking it and at what price: "The Prospectors
  stand at 47; they take this at 70." Hover it for the arithmetic. The old amber warning at
  the top of the card is gone - it was using out-of-date arithmetic.
- THE FACTION WINDOW SHOWS WHAT EACH SIDE HAS UNDER WAY: buildings begun and turns to go,
  ships in transit and where to. For rivals too.
- A VICTORY HISTORY CHART on every side's page: their progress turn by turn, with their
  blame share as a second line, and the big events ticked on the axis.
- EVENTS THAT CAN ONLY HAPPEN IN SPACE (dust storms, moonquakes, reactor leaks and so on)
  are held out of the deck until turn 12, so fewer cards are wasted early.
- ARMIES HAVE NAMES: the 1st Chinese Army, the 2nd Chinese Army, the Tycho Garrison.
- EACH SIDE HAS A MOTTO on its selection card.
- FIXED: a colony held by whoever went first was always drawn in Custodian teal, even when
  they were the Prospectors. An eleven-version-old bug, spotted in play.
- FIXED: the yields on the "Found a colony" button were words instead of pictures.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Spend it from the bar at the bottom right.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  what they have under way, what bargains stand between them - and where you smear them.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider (and the Prospectors' Fund slider).
- The TRADING window (M) buys and sells goods, and carbon credits.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- SMEAR: did you use it? On whom, and why? Did being smeared yourself feel unfair, or
  like part of the game? Was two ppm per influence worth it?
- CARBON CREDITS: did you buy any? Did the price make sense? If you played the Custodians,
  did you ever oversell on purpose?
- AGITATE: did you try it, and did anything happen? We suspect a single agitate is too
  weak against the natural calming and want to hear whether it ever felt worth 15 coins.
- THE CHALLENGER LINE: did it change what you spent influence on? Did the numbers agree
  with what actually happened when a rival took a place?
- THE RIVAL WARNING: did it fire? Did you know what to do about it? Did it fire too late?
- THE VICTORY HISTORY CHART: could you read it? Was the second line (blame) useful or noise?
- SEA WALLS: with walls now permanent, did you build them everywhere? Did the upkeep ever
  bite? Did you notice the storm-surge penalty?
- THE FUND SLIDER, if you played the Prospectors: is 2500 reachable? What share did you
  run at?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first.

- THE WIN COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. Out of eighty games: Prospectors 28, Custodians 26,
  Arkwrights 7, Archivists 0. Last version it was 30, 26, 8 and 1. Three of this version's
  four new weapons land on the Prospectors, and the fourth - agitate - turns out to be
  mostly THEIRS, because they have the coins. None of it dented them. The Archivists' one
  win became none, for last version's reasons: their way to win keeps their research out
  of the shared pool, and the 85% rule charges them for it every turn.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES A LITTLE MORE OFTEN: 19 games in 80, against 15. Every seating ends
  about a tenth of a degree hotter. Our best guess is that permanent sea walls keep
  coastal factories alive, and factories emit. Tell us whether the climate feels like a
  threat again.
- EVERYONE ENDS UP HATING EVERYONE, a bit more than before: 40% to 59% of relationships
  end scarred (41% to 48% last version), because smear and agitate are both offences and
  the computer uses both. Say if it felt that way.
- AGITATE ON ITS OWN IS WEAK. See above: unrest falls 1.5 a turn naturally, so one agitate
  a turn on a calm country nets out. Whether that calming should skip an agitated country
  is an open question; say what you think.
- THE COMPUTER CUSTODIANS SELL EVERY CREDIT THEY OFFER, and only the Prospectors (and a
  rich Arkwright side) ever buy. Nobody has been seen overselling.
- THE REPORT STILL SAYS "ARMY". Armies are named everywhere else - roster, cards, orders -
  but the turn report's own lines ("Custodian Army moved from China to India") do not use
  the name yet.
- SPACE EVENTS CAN STILL FIND NOTHING TO HIT: about three cards a game are drawn with
  nowhere to land, down from four. Most of what is left is Dust Storm, which needs someone
  living on Mars.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS WAS CUT from this version - it was a much bigger
  job than it looked. It may come back.
- TWO OF THE FOUR SPECIAL SPACE BUILDINGS ARE STILL NEVER BUILT BY THE COMPUTER (the
  Archivists' Heliostat and the Custodians' Academy). You can build either by hand.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION EVER GETS STRUCK between computer sides, and roughly three quarters
  of bargains are later broken. As last version.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods. As last
  version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are dealt the leftovers. The
  Prospectors win almost only when they go first or second.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
