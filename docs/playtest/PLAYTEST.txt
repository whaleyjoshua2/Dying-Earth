DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.6
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.08.5 will not load in this version; the Load screen says so. This version
   ships for Windows only.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.5
-------------------------
Last version made the war real. This one gives the defender a say, and rewrites what an
army IS.

- ONE ARMY SYSTEM. Every country has ONE army of its own, its standing army, which never
  leaves home. Its strength AND its hit points are the country's industry level plus one,
  plus every step it has "armed" by (see below). While it defends it fights harder: one
  more with a working constabulary, one more while unrest is under 4, and two more if it
  is dug in. None of that adds hit points. The card says "strength 4, damage 0/4,
  defends at 5".
- ARMIES YOU RAISE belong to the country you raised them in, change hands if it does,
  and are the only ones that march. Each is worth that country's industry level plus one,
  fixed for its life - so a 25-material army is a 2 in a poor country and a 4 in a rich
  one. Raise them where industry is highest. An army on a station or colony is worth the
  average industry of all the countries you hold, plus one.
- THE LEVY IS GONE. Instead, park an army next to a neutral country and its standing army
  gets +2 FOR GOOD, once per threat; a neutral that holds against an attack gets +1 for
  good; there is no ceiling. Neutral countries get harder and harder to take by force as
  the game goes on. Take them with influence instead - that price does not move.
- DIG IN is a new army stance beside Attack, Hold and Evade. A dug-in army fights two
  stronger while defending and never runs from a battle, but it cannot march or board a
  carrier until you change its stance and wait a turn. Neutral countries' armies are
  always dug in (a short line under their shield on the map says so).
- RUNNING FROM A BATTLE IS RARER. The chance a damaged unit slips away each round is a
  third smaller than it was. Evade is still a coin flip.
- CONQUEST BURNS LESS. A battle no longer burns a quarter of the buildings where it is
  fought. A country you occupy for three turns still loses a quarter of its buildings
  when it becomes yours - but a country you PACIFY (your standing there passes its
  threshold before the three turns are up) is taken whole, nothing lost. Beat the army,
  then win the people.
- AN OCCUPATION YOU ABANDON COSTS YOU. If your last army leaves an occupied country - by
  marching, by carrier, or by dying - the country goes back to whoever held it at +2
  unrest, you take an offence from them, and the standing the occupation had won you is
  wiped. Bring enough to finish the job.
- A LANDED ARMY ATTACKS THE TURN IT LANDS. Land an army from a carrier at a rival's colony
  and it fights the same turn (after any fight in orbit); if nobody defends, the
  occupation starts at once. The landing button now shows the odds.
- YOUR STATION STARTS WITH TWO COLONISTS ABOARD, so it can build two modules from turn
  one. The Arkwrights, who have no station, start with two pioneers waiting in their
  first country. The tutorial builds a habitat before it recruits.
- ORDERED BUILDINGS SHOW AT ONCE. Order a building and it appears in its box straight
  away, greyed and marked "ordered" with the turns to complete; after End Turn it says
  "building" and counts down. Right-click an ordered box to cancel it.
- SLIDERS for the smear, the greenwash and the influence spend on the command cluster,
  in single points from zero to everything you have this turn; the part already spent
  elsewhere is greyed. Max still spends it all in one press.
- THE COMMAND CLUSTER IS A TENTH LARGER, and End Turn is now the sun in the corner. Enter
  still works.
- THE TRADING WINDOW opens below the top bar instead of on top of it.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Set the amount on the slider at the bottom right
  and press Spend, or Max.
- ARMIES are raised from a region's card and march to a neighbouring region. Arriving in
  a region you do not hold is an attack. A region's own standing army never marches.
  Warships are built at a shipyard and fight in orbit; a warship in a rival station's
  slot can be ordered to Blockade.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  what they have under way, what bargains stand between them, where you smear them, and
  where you request carbon credits. Your own page is where you greenwash.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider (and the Prospectors' Fund slider).
- The TRADING window (R) buys and sells goods.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - the sun at the bottom right, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the
  temperature in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- THE ARMY ROW: does "strength 4, damage 0/4, defends at 5" make sense? Could you tell
  what an army would fight at before you attacked?
- DIG IN: did you use it? Did the turn it costs to dig out feel right?
- RAISING ARMIES: did you notice that where you raise one sets its strength? Did you
  move your army-building to your richest country?
- NEUTRALS ARMING: did a neutral country ever become too strong to take by force? Did you
  switch to influence?
- CONQUEST: did you ever pacify a country and keep everything? Did you ever abandon an
  occupation, and was the cost clear?
- THE LANDING: did you land an army at a rival's colony? Did the same-turn attack surprise
  you?
- THE STARTING STATION: did you build on it turn one? Did the tutorial's new order (habitat
  first) make sense?
- ORDERED BOXES and the RIGHT-CLICK cancel: did you find them?
- THE SLIDERS: better or worse than the number fields? Did the greyed part make sense?
- THE SUN: is End Turn still obvious?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first.

- THE WIN COLUMN MOVED A LONG WAY TOWARD THE CUSTODIANS. Out of eighty games: Custodians
  43, Prospectors 21, Arkwrights 2, Archivists 0. Last version it was 28, 26, 9 and 2.
  Every rule in this version hardens the defender, and the Prospectors were the side
  that attacked: the computer marched on a held country 5 times in eighty games where
  it marched 91, and took 2 places by force where it took 51. Without its conquests the
  Prospectors' money fell where they go first (614 a game against 3580) and the
  Custodians' rose everywhere. The Arkwrights' and Archivists' few wins last version came
  from the Prospectors' war, and went with it. Expect the next version to be about
  balance.
- THE COMPUTER NO LONGER WINS ON THE GROUND. It marches one army at a time and only at
  60% odds, and a single raised army now clears that against almost nobody. Two armies
  together would; it never masses. This is measured behaviour, not a rule: YOU can mass.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES ABOUT AS OFTEN: 14 games in 80, against 15 - but when the Arkwrights
  go first it collapsed 12 times in 20 where it collapsed 7, and that seating ends at
  +3.0 C. The other three end cooler than before. Tell us whether the climate still feels
  like a threat.
- THREE RULES NEVER FIRED IN EIGHTY COMPUTER GAMES: no occupation was abandoned, no army
  was landed at a colony, and no neutral country was ever threatened (every country is
  taken by influence before the first army exists). They will matter to YOU if you play
  that way.
- THE COMPUTER LOST A WARSHIP for the first time: one, of 228 built.
- THE BLOCKADE IS STILL MOSTLY THE PROSPECTORS' (107 colony-turns starved where they go
  second), though the Custodians and Arkwrights each blockaded a little this version.
- RELATIONS RUN WARMER than last version, because fewer battles are fewer offences: 42% to
  59% of relationships end scarred (45% to 62% before).
- THE REPORT STILL SAYS "ARMY" in its march lines.
- SPACE EVENTS CAN STILL FIND NOTHING TO HIT: about three cards a game.
- TWO OF THE FOUR SPECIAL SPACE BUILDINGS ARE STILL NEVER BUILT BY THE COMPUTER (the
  Archivists' Heliostat and the Custodians' Academy). You can build either by hand.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION EVER GETS STRUCK between computer sides, and most bargains are
  later broken. As last version.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods. As last
  version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are dealt the leftovers.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- On an 800-pixel-tall window the Faction window is taller than the screen and opens at
  the very top; on a taller window it sits under the top bar.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
