DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.09.9
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.09.8 load. Saves from 0.09.7 and before do not: the Load screen says so.
7. This version ships for Windows and for Linux. On Linux, read RUN-ON-LINUX.txt in the
   folder instead of steps 2 to 5.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.09.8
-------------------------
The settlement version: colonies grow through tiers you pay for, you can refuse what
other sides offer you, and the sides can trade. The computer plays every rule below.

SETTLING
- EVERY NEW COLONY OR STATION OPENS WITH FOUR PEOPLE, however it is made. A Colony Ship
  must set down four. Building a station from a country's Launch Site takes four Pioneers
  waiting there; building one from a Colony, or a Colony from a station, takes four of its
  people, and it must keep at least as many as its Modules. The Arkwrights start with four
  Pioneers waiting, so their first station can still go up on turn 1.
- COLONIES HAVE TIERS: Outpost, Settlement, Colony. Every place starts an Outpost. Its
  Module slots are its people, up to 6 for an Outpost, 12 for a Settlement, 18 for a
  Colony. The red "Upgrade" tile after the Module boxes buys the next tier: 30 Materials
  and 4 Widgets at 12 people, 50 and 6 at 18. The card's title says the tier.
- THE PROSPECTORS' EXCHANGE, their Trade Post off Earth, now also pays the Investment
  Bank's interest into their Fund.

BARGAINS
- YOU CAN REFUSE. An Accord another side offers you waits for your answer: a box at the
  start of your turn, Accept or Refuse, and the turn will not end until you answer. The
  computer sides answer theirs at the start of their next turn too, and a side you refuse
  waits three turns before asking again. Your own offers are answered the same way, and
  the Report tells you how it went.
- TRADES. On a rival's page of the Faction window, under Accords: give one thing for one
  thing, Ducats, Materials, Fuel, Energy, or one of your Colonies or stations. They answer
  next turn, and the goods move only if both of you still have them. A Colony traded
  changes hands peacefully, people and all. Not with a side that is Hostile. A trade
  counts as a kindness both ways.

THE MAP
- SOUTH AFRICA NOW RUNS UP THE EAST OF THE CONTINENT: Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda,
  Burundi, Ethiopia, Somalia and Djibouti joined it from Nigeria. Angola went back to
  Nigeria; Eritrea went to Egypt. South Africa now borders Egypt and faces Saudi Arabia
  across the Red Sea.

THE WINDOW
- STATIONS IN ORBIT are short rows: the station's name in its owner's colour, how many
  live there, and "blockaded" in red if one is. Hover a row for its Modules.
- A LINE now divides a Colony's card above its Modules.
- FOUR HOVERS ARE SHORTER: a world's In orbit heading, Greenwash, a Ship in your roster,
  and the Modules heading.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- TIERS: did you find the Upgrade tile? Did the six-Module cap of an Outpost stop you?
- OFFERS: did the Accept / Refuse box come at a sensible moment? Did you refuse anything?
- TRADES: did you trade? With whom, and what? Did you ever trade a Colony?
- FOUR TO FOUND: did needing four people slow your first Colony?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. These are known. You do not need to report them.

WHAT THIS VERSION KNOWS ABOUT ITSELF

- THE WIN COLUMN: Custodians 2, Prospectors 23, Arkwrights 30, Archivists 2, where last
  version was 9 / 27 / 18 / 7. Four people to found a Colony moved it most: stations off
  Earth are much rarer, and the Arkwrights, whose ships carry eight, reach the Moon first
  far more often. Why the Custodians stopped winning is not yet traced.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 20 GAMES OF 80, where it was 17.
- THE ARCHIVISTS SCORE NOTHING at the end in 50 games of 80.
- FEW PLACES CLIMB A TIER: at the end of the eighty games, 884 Outposts, 87 Settlements
  and 41 Colonies.
- THE COMPUTER OFFERS MANY TRADES AND FEW ARE STRUCK: about 20 a game, one in seven
  accepted, mostly because the side asked is short of the same thing.
- THE COMPUTER NEVER TRADES A COLONY, and never offers one.
- THE COMPUTER NEVER USES EARTH L4 OR L5, AND NEVER BUILDS A MASS DRIVER. Both are yours.
- A COMPUTER SIDE STARTS IN AUSTRALIA AGAIN, where one started in Saudi Arabia: Saudi
  Arabia now borders South Africa, which another computer side takes.
- THE EXCHANGE'S BUILD HOVER RUNS TO SEVEN LINES, one over the limit.

CARRIED OVER, STILL TRUE

- THE COMPUTER BUILDS FEW REFINERIES OFF EARTH, so the better refining on the small moons is
  mostly yours.
- THE COMPUTER ALMOST NEVER BUILDS A SHIPYARD ON THE MOON, PHOBOS OR DEIMOS.
- TWO STACKS THAT ATTACK IN ONE ORBIT IN ONE TURN FIGHT TWO BATTLES, one per attack.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the same countries
  in the same order.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
