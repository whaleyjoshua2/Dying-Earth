DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.09.2
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.09.1 and before will not load in this version: battles are recorded and
   fought differently now and every side starts differently, so an old file describes a
   board this version cannot read. The Load screen says so.
7. This version ships for Windows and for Linux. On Linux, read RUN-ON-LINUX.txt in the
   folder instead of steps 2 to 5.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.09.1
-------------------------
Most of this version is tidying: the game says what it does where you can see it, and
the ship cards were rebuilt. Three rules moved, and the computer plays every one.

THE RULES THAT MOVED
- AN ATTACK IS FOUGHT THE MOMENT YOU CONFIRM IT. Order a ship stack to Attack and the
  battle happens at once, on the board as it stands - ships still in flight toward that
  world do not arrive in time to join. The survivors stand on Hold, and a stack fights
  once a turn. The computer's attacks are fought when it gives its orders, before
  anything moves. "Confirm Attack" is the point of no return; you cannot save until the
  next turn after a battle.
- EVERY BATTLE HAS A WINDOW OF ITS OWN, shown the moment yours is fought and at the start
  of the next turn for the computer's. It draws each round: every side's ships with a pip
  per hit point, the hits of the round lit, a ship that ran dimmed, one destroyed
  crossed, and the blows in words ("The Custodians hit ARK Implacable, covering ARK
  Defiant"). The chronicle at the end of the game lists every battle that cost a ship or
  a battery.
- EQUAL STARTS. Every home country now begins with the same buildings - a Power Plant, a
  Factory, a Mine, a Refinery and the Launch Site - whatever it had on the map, and every
  starting station carries a Solar Array. The Arkwrights, who start with no station, get a
  second Power Plant instead. The country's ECONOMY is still its own: the EU pays about
  twelve Ducats a turn and Saudi Arabia one.
- THE REFUGEE CONVOY LANDS A MILLION PEOPLE for half a ppm (it was 400,000 for two).
- NO CARD IS DRAWN ON THE FIRST TURN.

THE SHIP CARDS
- EVERY SHIP HAS A CARD OF ITS OWN: its strength and damage, its tank, its moves, its
  cargo, its weapons and its repair. Click a ship's row on the stack card, in the roster,
  or its label on the solar map while it flies. The stack card keeps only what is the
  whole stack's: the ships grouped by orbit, the stance, Attack, and "All that can".
- WHERE A SHIP CAN GO IS ONE DROP-DOWN PER WORLD, the moons nested under their planets -
  the Moon inside Earth's, Phobos and Deimos inside Mars's - with the orbits inside each.
  The world you are at opens first with the change-of-orbit moves.
- A TRANSIT QUOTE SAYS WHEN IT HOLDS ("if launched this turn; next turn 5t/23F, then
  6t/26F"), and a leg that would leave the ship stranded at the far end says so beside its
  button. A warning, not a refusal: a one-way trip can be the plan.

THE GAME SAYS WHAT IT DOES
- EVERY GREYED BUTTON EXPLAINS WHY ON MOUSEOVER, in the engine's own words, the reason
  first and the description after. (It turned out no greyed order button had shown its
  reason since version 0.08.3; the pictures we checked looked right because the screenshot
  tool forced them open. Fixed.)
- THE VICTORY BAR COUNTS SETTLED COLONISTS, with a hatched band beyond the fill for the
  ones still in flight.
- THE REPORT HAS A SHIPS HEADING: colonists waiting aboard in orbit, ships held, ships
  loaded. One net Unrest line per country instead of several.
- A CARD'S INFLUENCE FIGURE READS THE HOLDER: "Take at 49" on a rival's place, the
  cheapest rival's price on your own.
- THE TRADING WINDOW SPEAKS IN GLYPHS. The "first to land" line is off the solar map and
  on the world's hover.
- THE ARCHIVISTS AND THE PROSPECTORS SEE THEIR FUND ON THE TOP BAR: the slider and the
  fill bar, condensed, right of the window buttons. The full controls stay where they were.
- THE DISTRESS CALL NAMES THE SHIP IT WOULD HOLD before you answer, and holds it for the
  turn if you do.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- ATTACK AT ONCE: did fighting the moment you confirm feel right? Did you want to take an
  attack back? Did the battle window tell you why you won or lost?
- THE SHIP CARDS: did you find a ship's moves? Were the nested drop-downs clear?
- THE REFUSAL HOVERS: hover over a greyed button. Did it tell you what to do?
- EQUAL STARTS: did the opening feel fair? Which country did you pick, and why?
- Anything that still says the same thing twice.
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first. These are known. You do not need to report them.

WHAT THIS VERSION KNOWS ABOUT ITSELF

- THE WIN COLUMN TURNED OVER: Custodians 2, Prospectors 5, Arkwrights 0, Archivists 11,
  where last version was 6 / 28 / 3 / 2. Equal starts did it: the Archivists' station
  carries a Heliostat and their home a Reactor, and they were first to the Moon in every
  game; the Prospectors' opening lost its habitat to an Exchange in the one slot the Solar
  Array leaves. Every side is stronger; the Prospectors' lead is gone.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES IN 62 GAMES OF 80, where it was 41. Every side now starts with a
  Refinery and a Power Plant it may not have had, and the table burns faster. The balance
  version will look at it.
- TWO STACKS THAT ATTACK IN ONE ORBIT IN ONE TURN FIGHT TWO BATTLES, one per attack, the
  second against the first's survivors. Deliberate: every attack is its own fight now.
- A COMMS BLACKOUT CANNOT STOP AN ATTACK YOU FOUGHT BEFORE THE TURN ENDED. It still stands
  the computer's stacks down.
- AN ARKWRIGHTS PLAYER WHO OPENS IN MEXICO has six buildings in five slots and nothing free
  to build until an Industry Level raise.
- NOBODY CAN ACCEPT OR DECLINE AN ACCORD OFFERED TO THEM - INCLUDING YOU. The game still
  answers on your behalf.
- THE THREAT LINE ON A COUNTRY'S CARD STILL QUOTES THE FIRST EXCHANGE, while every other
  odds figure is the whole battle's. Deliberate, until both move together.

CARRIED OVER, STILL TRUE

- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the same countries
  in the same order.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
