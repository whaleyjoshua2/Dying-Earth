DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.07.0
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
   (Windows only this time; there is no Linux zip for 0.07.0.)

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.06.0
-------------------------
This version came out of twelve games played against 0.06.0 by computer players told to
try to win. They found the game could be broken, so most of this is repair.

- COLONIES AND STATIONS NOW HAVE A SIZE. A colony or station holds 3 buildings free and
  one more for every colonist living there. Before this there was no limit at all, and a
  single Moon colony could grow to 79 mines and generators and out-earn a whole planet.
  People now gate industry, so habitats matter: a full one always pays for itself and
  more.
- THE COMPUTER RESEARCHES NOW. It built almost no laboratories before, which meant it
  never reached the technology its own victory needs. It does now, and computer players
  win about a third of games instead of none.
- THE TECH CHOICE IS A CHOICE OF THREE. Whoever leads research picks the next technology
  from three, not from everything - and YOUR OWN victory technology is always one of the
  three once you can reach it, so nobody can lock you out of your own win. The first
  technology of the game is still a free pick from all of rung 1.
- THE TURN WILL NOT END until you have picked a technology when it is your turn to. A
  box tells you why, with a button to the tech tree.
- WARSHIPS BLOCKADE ONE ORBITAL SLOT, NOT A WHOLE WORLD. Give a slot when you fly -
  "send to Mars, slot 2" - and a warship sitting there shuts that one station: nobody
  else may unload into it, refuel from it, or build there. Everything else at that world
  carries on, and nobody can ever shut you out of a place you hold. The ground is only
  closed to you if a rival holds the orbit outright and uncontested.
- THE START SCREEN'S GLOBE WORKS. Drag to turn it, wheel to zoom, click a continent to
  take it. It opens on your side's home: the Custodians in Europe, the Prospectors in
  East Asia, the Arkwrights in South Asia, the Archivists in North America. That is only
  where the globe starts; you may still take any of the twelve.
- ANTARCTICA IS NOT ON THE MAP until the ice opens. You are still told it is coming and
  at what temperature, on the system map and the climate bar - you just cannot see which
  of the three sites is the good one until you can go there.
- NOTHING OPENS ITSELF. A new game starts on a clear map; the tech tree and climate panel
  wait to be asked for. The climate panel can be dragged smaller again, and says "Already
  locked in" where it used to say "Committed".
- THE RESOURCES HAVE PICTURES, on the top bar and in the hover text. They come from
  game-icons.net; the Credits button on the title screen names their authors.
- LEAPFROG IS WORTH BUYING. It now lowers a country's baseline pollution as well as its
  people's, so it is no longer ten times the price of a scrubber for the same good. The
  scrubber also runs on 3 energy instead of 4.
- THE REPORT IS SHORTER by about an eighth, all of it cut from the computer telling you
  it had done nothing.

HOW TO PLAY, IN ONE MINUTE
--------------------------
- New Game > pick one of the four sides > pick a starting continent, from the list or by
  clicking the globe. The computer plays the other three. There is also a Spectate button
  under the cards: the computer plays all four and you just watch.
- You are looking at the Earth Map. Click a country for its card and orders on the right.
  Drag to turn the globe, mouse wheel to zoom. Tab switches to the Solar System Map;
  Esc goes back.
- Build things with the buttons on a card. Hover a build button to see the cost and what
  it makes.
- Spend Influence on other countries to win them over. Your home country starts held.
- Every country has an "Unrest" number. Heat, floods and refugees push it up. At 4 its
  army stops growing, at 7 its factories work at half speed, and at 10 the country throws
  you out. Pay Relief, build a Constabulary, or muster Emigrants to push it back down.
- If you are the Custodians, build Scrubbers: they cost no building space, they pull
  carbon back out of the air, and they calm the country they stand in. Leapfrog is worth
  a look now too.
- Open the Climate Panel (the C key). The coloured bar marks every point where the world
  changes for good, including where Antarctica opens.
- Getting off Earth: put a Shipyard on your station first - ships are only built at a
  Shipyard. Muster Emigrants in a country with a Launch Site, lift them, and load them on
  a Colony Ship. The Moon is one turn away. Mars is cheap only in a launch window; hover
  Mars to see when. Remember a colony's size limit: land people before you plan buildings.
- End Turn (top left, red). Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature in the top bar:
  at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- A rule you could not find explained anywhere.
- DOES THE COLONY SIZE LIMIT FEEL RIGHT? Three free and one per colonist. Too tight, too
  loose, or about right? It is the biggest change in this version.
- Did you ever want to blockade someone, and did picking the slot when you fly make
  sense, or did you want to move a ship between slots after it arrived?
- Did the shortlist of three technologies feel like a real choice or a lottery?
- CAN YOU DRAG THE CLIMATE PANEL SMALLER? We could not test that automatically, so it is
  the one change nobody has actually watched work.
- Do the resource pictures in the hover text read clearly, or would you rather have the
  words back?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
- THE CUSTODIANS ARE TOO STRONG. Over eighty computer-only games they win 49, from any
  seat, and 20 of 20 from Europe. Their win was impossible before this version and is now
  close to certain; that is the next thing to fix.
- THE ARCHIVISTS WIN NOTHING in those eighty, though the Archive itself now gets built
  and finished. Getting twelve people to live at it is the part that fails.
- Every side's victory target was set against a computer that never competed for it, so
  the numbers are all suspect. A player who tries can clear three of the four well before
  turn 36.
- The world still collapses in about a fifth of games, more from some starting continents
  than others. Some of that is meant to be there.
- The Climate Panel's drag-smaller and the pictures in the hover text are the two changes
  that could not be tested without a person; if either misbehaves, please say.
- No sound, no tutorial beyond the first Report.
