DYING EARTH - playtest build, version 0.08.5
============================================

Thanks for playing. This is an early build of a turn-based strategy game about colonizing
the solar system before global warming wrecks Earth. It is unfinished and unbalanced on
purpose: the point of this playtest is to find out what is confusing, what is boring,
and what breaks.

HOW TO RUN
----------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (Desktop is fine). Keep dying-earth.exe and the
   "assets" folder together; the game reads its rules and pictures from there.
2. Double-click dying-earth.exe.
3. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box because the program is not
   signed. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. The very first launch shows a blank dark window for up to ten seconds while it
   compiles graphics. Later launches are quick.
5. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and any graphics card from the last ten years.
6. Saves from 0.08.4 will not load in this version; the Load screen says so. This version
   ships for Windows only.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE 0.08.4
-------------------------
Last version was about what the sides can do to each other with influence and money.
This one is about the war: it is louder, it is dirtier, and the computer is finally
allowed to start one.

- THE COMPUTER CAN ATTACK ANY COUNTRY, INCLUDING YOURS. Until now a computer side only
  marched on a country it had lost. Now any side attacks a neutral country when the odds
  are good, and a country you hold when the odds are good AND they are Cold toward you
  or worse. Keep your neighbours friendly, or keep an army. The computer opens at most
  one new war a turn.
- BATTLES POLLUTE. Every hit landed in a battle on Earth or over it puts half a ppm of
  carbon in the air, and every building burned in a conquest puts two. It counts as
  emissions for whoever landed the hits, so it goes on their blame and it breaks a
  stabilisation run. Fighting over Mars fouls nobody's air. The Climate panel (C) has a
  "War" line when there is any.
- BATTLE REPORTS. A battle is now its own line in the turn report, at its place, and it
  interrupts the turn when a ship or an army is destroyed. It names every ship and army
  that fought, what each one took ("PMV Aurora escaped after 3 hits"), and the odds the
  attacker faced in the first round. Armies that die are named in the report too.
- NEUTRAL COUNTRIES ARM WHEN THREATENED. Park an army next to a country nobody holds and
  it raises a second army, a levy, at its industry level plus two, as long as it is not
  too restless to muster. The levy never marches and stands down when you go away. A
  neutral country that is attacked and holds gets +1 to its standing army for good, up
  to +3. And a standing army you destroy now takes two turns to come back, not one.
- A BLOCKADE STARVES. A blockade is now an ORDER you give a warship (a stance, like Hold
  or Attack), not something that happens because your ship is parked there. A blockaded
  station makes nothing - no goods, no research, no coins - and still pays its upkeep.
  A colony on the ground starves the same way when one rival owns the orbit above it
  outright and has a warship there on Blockade. Nobody dies. Each turn of it is a small
  offence against the owner.
- GREENWASH. On your own page in the Faction window (F) you can spend influence and coins
  to take blame off your own ledger: one influence and one coin per two ppm. No carbon
  actually leaves the air. It is the mirror of the smear, and everyone can see you did it.
- CARBON CREDITS MOVED. They are no longer in the Trading window. If you are the
  Custodians you set your offer on your own page of the Faction window; everyone else
  requests them from the Custodians' page there, under the accords and the smear.
- THE SEA REACHES INLAND. Every sea-level rise now also turns one inland building slot
  into a coastal one, in every country, whether or not it has a sea wall. The wall stops
  the sea taking a slot; it does not stop the coast creeping in. An empty slot turns
  first; if none is empty, the oldest inland building becomes a coastal one. There is no
  floor: a country can end up all coast.
- BUILDINGS SAY WHAT THEY DO. A school, a launch site, a constabulary and every other
  building that makes no resource now says what it is for on its row, instead of "no
  output".
- THE PLANET CARD LISTS COLONIES AND SITES WITH THEIR YIELDS as pictures, and the "found
  a colony" panel no longer repeats the planet-wide yields - the planet card has them
  at its head instead.

HOW TO PLAY, IN SHORT
---------------------
- Pick a side on the start screen, then pick where on Earth you begin. Each side wins a
  different way; its card says how.
- Click a region or a colony to select it. Its card appears on the right.
- A region's buildings are the row of boxes on its card. Click an empty box to build.
- A colony's buildings are the grid of tiles on its card. Click an empty tile to build.
- INFLUENCE is how you win regions over. Spend it from the bar at the bottom right.
- ARMIES are built from a region's card and march to a neighbouring region. Arriving in
  a region you do not hold is an attack. Warships are built at a launch site and fight
  in orbit; a warship in a rival station's slot can be ordered to Blockade.
- The FACTION WINDOW (F) has a page for each side: what they have, what they think of you,
  what they have under way, what bargains stand between them, where you smear them, and
  where you request carbon credits. Your own page is where you greenwash.
- The VICTORY panel (V) shows how far each side is along its own path, and holds your
  Research Directive slider (and the Prospectors' Fund slider).
- The TRADING window (M) buys and sells goods.
- The TECH TREE (T) is shared: everyone's research goes into the same tree unless they
  keep some back.
- The roster on the right lists everything you own. An open ring at the end of a row
  means it is still waiting for an order from you this turn.
- End Turn - bottom right, red, or press Enter. Thirty-six turns. Watch the temperature
  in the top bar: at +3.0 C the world collapses and nobody wins.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW
--------------------
Write down anything, however small. Especially:
- The moment you were not sure what to do next.
- Anything you clicked that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
- BEING ATTACKED: did the computer ever march on a country you held? Did you see it
  coming? Did the Relations gate (they must be Cold toward you) make sense, or did it
  feel random?
- THE BATTLE REPORT: could you tell who fought whom and who lost? Was the "odds" figure
  useful? Did the interruption fire when you wanted it to, or too often?
- BATTLES POLLUTE: did you notice the War line on the Climate panel? Did it ever change
  a decision about fighting?
- NEUTRAL LEVIES: did you ever see a country raise one? Did it stop you attacking, or
  did you attack anyway? Did the +1 for holding feel earned?
- THE BLOCKADE: did you order one? Did you suffer one? Was it clear on the station's card
  what was happening and who was doing it?
- GREENWASH: did you use it? Was two ppm per influence-and-coin worth it, and did it feel
  like cheating?
- CARBON CREDITS: could you find them in their new place? Was the "request" button clear?
- THE SEA REACHING INLAND: did you notice the coastal boxes growing? Did it change where
  you built, or whether you built a wall?
- The turn the game got boring, if it did, and the turn you felt you had lost.
- Crashes: what you were doing, and the "Seed" number shown on the title screen and
  the game-over screen. With the seed the game can be replayed exactly.

Screenshots help. Print Screen, then paste into a message.

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
All the numbers below come from eighty computer-only games, twenty with each side going
first.

- THE WIN COLUMN MOVED, A LITTLE, AND TOWARD THE CUSTODIANS. Out of eighty games:
  Custodians 28, Prospectors 26, Arkwrights 9, Archivists 2. Last version it was 26, 28,
  7 and 0. The war is a money sink for whoever has the money - the Prospectors built 46
  armies and 72 warships across the twenty games where they go second, and made a third
  less coin doing it - and when the Prospectors go first the Custodians now win 5 of 20
  where they won none. The Archivists have two wins for the first time since 0.08.3.
- THE WORLD COLLAPSES LESS OFTEN: 15 games in 80, against 19. Every seating ends a bit
  cooler. Our guess is that the sea reaching inland drowns more buildings' work over a
  game (27 to 36 coastal slots lost a game, against 22 to 29), and a starved station
  makes nothing. Tell us whether the climate still feels like a threat.
- THE WAR IS ONE SIDE'S. Whoever starts in East Asia with the coins fights: the
  Prospectors opened 57 and 49 battles a batch when they hold it, the Arkwrights 52 when
  they do. Every other seat and seating together opened fourteen. No battle in eighty
  games was against a neutral country: every country is taken by influence in the first
  turns, and the first army appears around turn 20, so the levy rule fired only three
  times in eighty games and never stopped an attack. It will matter to YOU if you march
  early; the computer does not.
- THE COMPUTER NEVER LOSES A WARSHIP. A hundred and twenty-nine were built over the batch
  and none was destroyed, the same as last version: the computer's warships blockade and
  rarely meet another warship that fights back.
- THE BLOCKADE IS THE PROSPECTORS' ALONE, and only when they go second: 139 colony-turns
  starved in that seating, most of them the Archivists' and Arkwrights' stations, and
  none anywhere else. We plan a counter (an orbital battery) in a later version.
- THE CREDIT TRADE ROSE, NOT FELL: the Prospectors buy 1214 to 1740 ppm of credit a batch
  (733 to 1408 before) AND greenwash 70 to 90 ppm a game on top. Both levers together
  take a fifth off their blame at the end (669 against 813 where they go second). The
  Custodians sell every credit they offer, still.
- EVERYONE HATES EVERYONE A LITTLE MORE: 45% to 62% of relationships end scarred (40% to
  59% last version). War, blockade and smear are all offences.
- ONLY THE PROSPECTORS EVER GREENWASH. The other computer sides are rarely dirty enough
  to want to.
- THE REPORT STILL SAYS "ARMY" in its march lines ("Custodian Army moved from China to
  India"), though battle lines and destroyed armies use the army's name now.
- SPACE EVENTS CAN STILL FIND NOTHING TO HIT: about three cards a game.
- TWO OF THE FOUR SPECIAL SPACE BUILDINGS ARE STILL NEVER BUILT BY THE COMPUTER (the
  Archivists' Heliostat and the Custodians' Academy). You can build either by hand.
- ONLY NON-AGGRESSION EVER GETS STRUCK between computer sides, and most bargains are
  later broken. As last version.
- FUEL AND ENERGY PRICES BARELY MOVE, because the computer sides never sell goods. As last
  version.
- WHERE EACH SIDE STARTS IS NOT RANDOM. The computer sides always take the richest free
  countries in the same order, so the third and fourth sides are dealt the leftovers.
- RENAMING SHIPS, COLONIES AND STATIONS is still cut, from last version.
- A station ring can be edge-on for a moment as you turn the globe; it comes back as
  you keep turning.
- The list of station slots on the Solar System Map appears only while you point at
  the world, because open all the time it covered the Moon.
- A region's name at the very edge of the globe on the start screen can sit half under
  the panel. Turn the globe a little.
- Antarctica's three colony sites have labels that overlap each other when you are zoomed
  out.
- No sound.
