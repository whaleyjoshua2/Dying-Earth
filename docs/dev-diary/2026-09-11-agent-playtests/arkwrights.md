### The pipeline, measured

One order per ship per turn is the whole constraint. A full cycle is:

| Destination | Ship-turns per load | Colonists per ship-turn |
|---|---|---|
| A station over Earth | 2 (load, unload) | **6-8** |
| The Moon | 4 (load, transit, unload, transit home) | **3-4** |
| Mars at a window | 8 one way (load, transit, 5 flight, unload), 6+ to come home | **~1.5** |

Game 3's actual ledger: ship 17 built turn 4, delivered 12 Colonists to two Bodies in ten
ship-turns; ship 19 built turn 9, delivered 24 Colonists to the Moon in seven ship-turns.
**Two ships, 36 Colonists, three Bodies, sixteen turns.** The pipeline is slow but it is
not the wall - the Research gate was, in all three games. If you have one ship it hurts;
the fix is a second ship, which costs 25 Materials and 30 Fuel.

# Findings

Three games: **won on turn 25, turn 20 and turn 16** of 36. Seeds 20260911 (twice) and
20260912; starts South Asia (twice) and East Asia.

## What held across every seed and start

1. **The Arkwrights win, early, and nothing contests it.** Wins on turns 25, 20 and 16.
   The best rival score at the end of any of the three games was 0.41; two games ended
   with all three rivals on 0.00.
2. **No rival ever reached its Victory gate**, because I held the Research Lead and never
   picked one. Game 1: I led 10 of 13 Techs. Game 2: 9 of 9. Game 3: 6 of 8. The
   Custodians ended game 1 with eight Nation States, five Colonies and 37 Colonists off
   Earth against a bar of 12, and scored **zero**.
3. **Research is so scarce the Lead is cheap to monopolise.** Winning margins: 6-6, 6-6,
   9-6, 12-6, 15-0, 18-0, 21-6, 30-6. Three or four Observatories - 63 to 84 Materials -
   bought the Lead for a whole game.
4. **Steerage empties its own Nation State in about ten turns.** South Asia 19.6 to 2.6
   population by turn 12; East Asia 16.4 to 1.6 by turn 11, after which
   `emigrants eastasia 8` was refused outright. In every game I banked far more Emigrants
   than the victory needed (72, 40, 52 against a requirement of about 36).
5. **The launch windows are on a fixed calendar.** The sky is real, so Mars opens on
   turn 7 and turn 20 and Venus on turn 9 and about turn 19 **in every game, on every
   seed**. Turns 7 to 10 are a single window in which every Body in the game sits inside
   a 30 tank at once (`from Earth: Mars 5t/16F | Phobos 5t/19F | Deimos 5t/19F |
   Venus 3t/13F`). Miss it and the next chance is turn 20.
6. **The window panel contradicts the flying table** on both seeds (see suggestion 7).
7. **I never used the Refuel order, never built a Mass Driver, Trade Post, Relay,
   Barracks, Bank or Embassy, and never deliberately took a crowded load** - in any of
   the three games. Fuel was never the binding constraint; I finished with 36, 55 and
   88 spare.
8. **The Custodians run away with the board and cannot convert it.** They took every
   Nation State, every Earth station and most Colonies in all three games, and their
   Stabilization run never left 0/3.

## What varied by seed and start

- **The sea eating the Launch Site.** On seed 20260911 the sea took South Asia's Launch
  Site on turn 16 and turn ~14 with 60 and 28 Emigrants stranded and no free slot to
  rebuild it - the single best moment in either game. On seed 20260912 from East Asia it
  never happened: the sea stripped East Asia's coastal slots on turn 3, before the Launch
  Site was exposed. This is a South-Asia-shaped hazard, not a general one.
- **Ducats.** Worthless from South Asia: 6 Ducats after 25 turns and 0 after 20, because
  gdp x Industry Level / 10 floors to zero at gdp 3 and 4. From East Asia (gdp 23) it was
  6 a turn from turn 1 and I twice bought Energy to avoid a shutdown. The Trading window
  is a real system for two of the twelve states and a dead letter for the rest.
- **Collapse timing.** Projected turn 36, turn 32 and turn 26 in the three games; never
  actually reached. Clean Manufacturing alone moved world emissions from 41.2 to 25.0 in
  game 1 and pushed collapse from turn 29 to turn 36 in a single step.
- **How the win was reached.** Game 2 met 15 of its 39 Colonists in a single Habitat in
  low Earth orbit; game 3 delivered all 36 to real Bodies and was *four turns faster*,
  because it caught the turn-7 Mars window. Both routes work; the orbital one is a
  fallback for a player who misses the window, not the only path.

## Balance

The Diaspora bar is not the obstacle. What thirty Colonists cost me:

| Piece | Cost | Colonists it is worth |
|---|---|---|
| One Habitat on a station over Earth | 18 Materials, 1 turn, 0 Fuel | **15** |
| Founding any Colony (a free Habitat comes with it) | 0 extra | 12-19 |
| Colony Ship | 25 Materials + 30 Fuel | carries 12, then 16 |

Thirty Colonists off Earth is two Habitats in low Earth orbit - 36 Materials and no space
travel at all, because 0.06.0 made a station over Earth count as off it. The three-Bodies
clause is the only part with teeth, and one ship on the turn-7 Mars window satisfies two
of the three Bodies by itself (Mars, then a 1-Fuel hop to Phobos).

**The gate is the whole game.** Generation Ships needs Expanded Habitats, Efficient Grids,
Clean Power and Closed-Loop Colonies first - 120 Research, and the last two are on no
other Faction's pick list. It landed on turns 18, 16 and 14. In every game I was winning
on paper before the ships had finished moving.

Numbers that felt wrong, and by how much:
- A Habitat over Earth is worth 15 Colonists for 18 Materials in two ship-turns. A Moon
  run is worth 12 for 25 Materials, 30 Fuel and four ship-turns. The orbital Habitat is
  about **five times** better per Material.
- Steerage musters roughly **twice** what the Faction can ever use, and costs its state
  about **twice** what it can afford.
- A Nation State below about gdp 10 pays **zero** Ducats. Nine of the twelve states are
  below it.

## Engagement

**Real decisions:** turns 1-2 (all three rivals dogpiled North America on turn 1; reading
that in the Report and quietly walking away was the best-designed moment in the game).
Turn 9 of game 1, the first Research Lead on a 6-6 tiebreak. Turns 16-17 of game 1, losing
the Launch Site and having to find another way to lift people. Turns 7-10 of any game: one
ship, four Bodies reachable, and a decision about which one.

**Autopilot:** turns 3-8 of game 1 were four Solar Arrays in a fixed order. Turns 21-25 of
game 1 were watching timers. **Turns 5, 8 and 19 of game 1 I had no affordable build at
all** - 12, 19 and 21 Materials against a cheapest useful building of 15, 21 and 25.
Income of 12-18 Materials a turn against buildings of 18-35 means every third turn is
spent banking, and all I did was type the Emigrant line and the Influence line.

**Influence goes stale.** Once I held every reachable state, Standing kept piling up past
the threshold (South America read 63/37) with nowhere useful to go.

**Tanks, refuelling and windows.** The tank is a good rule but it decides only twice a
game: 30 Fuel against Mars at 35-47 off-window makes the far Bodies strictly binary. You
go on the window or you never go, and the window is a fixed calendar, so after one game it
is a schedule rather than a judgement. **No ship was ever stranded in three games**,
because the `check` command refuses an unaffordable leg - the trap can only spring on
someone who does not read the table. I never used Refuel once.

## Enjoyment

**Satisfying:** the first Research Lead on a tiebreak. Antarctica - a free Colony with a
free Habitat, reached by sea with no ship, no Fuel and no Launch Site, and a Mine there
paying back in under two turns. The Venus loop (fly a loaded ship there, build the station
*from the ship in orbit*, put a Habitat on it, unload) is the best-designed thing in
0.06.0 and read exactly as written.

**Tedious:** banking Materials for two turns to afford one building. Solar Array, Solar
Array, Solar Array. The Influence phase after turn 15. Watching the AI print "N Emigrants
found no room in Antarctica and came home" in six consecutive turns.

**Confusing:** nothing on the board ever showed **any Faction's Standing on a Colony or a
station**; "Last income" is the previous turn's, so it never matches what you are about to
be paid; and a Colony founded from a ship silently comes with a free Habitat, which is
load-bearing and written down nowhere.

## Concrete suggestions, ranked

Each is: what I observed, an option or two, and the trade-off. The designer decides.

**1. The Research Lead lets one seat lock every rival out of winning, for good.**
Observed in all three games: I led 9 of 9, 10 of 13 and 6 of 8 Techs and simply never
picked a rival gate. The Custodians finished game 1 with eight states, five Colonies and
37 Colonists off Earth, and scored 0.00.
*Options:* (a) let a Faction's own gate become available to that Faction alone, paid from
its own Research, without needing the pick; (b) give the second-placed contributor the
pick every other Tech; (c) auto-open every gate from some late turn.
*Trade-off:* (a) and (c) take most of the bite out of the Lead, which is one of the better
tensions in the game; (b) keeps the race but makes it harder for a player to read.

**2. Colonists on a station over Earth count toward Diaspora's thirty.**
Observed game 2 turn 20: 15 of 39 were in one 18-Material Habitat that never left orbit.
*Options:* (a) let a station over Earth count for Off-world Presence but not for the
Arkwrights' thirty; (b) count them at half.
*Trade-off:* (a) gives the Arkwrights a different reading of "off Earth" from everyone
else, which is a wrinkle, but it restores the point of the card; (b) is one rule for all
but fiddly, and orbital Habitats are still the cheapest Colonists in the game.

**3. Research output is far too small, so the Lead is bought with four Observatories.**
Observed: Expanded Habitats completed on turn 6 of game 1 with the leader on 8 points in
total; my winning margins ran to 30-6.
*Options:* (a) raise everyone's Research substantially so the Lead swings between seats;
(b) leave output alone and raise Tech costs so no single burst decides a Tech.
*Trade-off:* (a) makes Techs arrive faster and shortens the game; (b) keeps the pace but
makes an already static early game more static.

**4. Nothing shows a rival's Standing on a Colony or a station.**
Observed game 1 turn 24: the Custodians took my only station with no warning and I lost a
Shipyard, two Habitats, four Solar Arrays and two Observatories in one Report line; Energy
income went from +11 to -23. I then built a replacement and lost it the turn its Habitat
finished - 38 Materials for nothing. In games 2 and 3, 5 to 10 Influence a turn on the
same station held it easily. The counter-play exists and is cheap; only the information is
missing.
*Options:* (a) show every Faction's Standing on each Colony and station, with the same
orange warning Nation States get; (b) make a Colony harder to take - a larger challenge
margin, or require a Colonist or a Ship present.
*Trade-off:* (a) is pure information and looks right on its own; (b) changes a mechanic
that is otherwise working.

**5. Steerage musters far more people than the Arkwrights can use, and guts its state.**
Observed: South Asia 19.6 to 2.6 by turn 12; East Asia refused to muster at all by turn 11.
*Options:* (a) six a turn at 1.5x population instead of eight at 2x; (b) cap the Emigrants
a state may hold un-lifted.
*Trade-off:* (a) weakens a signature rule that is not the Faction's problem anyway;
(b) adds something more for the player to track.

**6. Losing your only Launch Site can be unrecoverable.**
Observed on seed 20260911, both games: the sea took South Asia's Launch Site at +2.2 C
with 60 and 28 Emigrants stranded, and the state had zero free build slots because the sea
had taken those too. Recovery cost 30 Materials for an Industry Level, then 30 for the
Launch Site, three turns.
*Options:* (a) let the Launch Site take no build slot, as the Sea Wall and Scrubber do;
(b) let Emigrants move to a neighbouring state you direct.
*Trade-off:* (a) removes a genuinely good disaster from the game; (b) keeps the disaster
and gives it an answer, at the cost of one more order.

**7. The launch window panel contradicts the flying table.**
Observed game 2 turn 8 and game 3 turn 8. Panel: "Mars window: in 11 turns ... Flight now:
6 turns, 27 Fuel. At the window: 5 turns, 20 Fuel." Table two lines above:
`from Earth: Mars 5t/16F`. I flew for 16 on a turn the panel called dear and off-window.
The table is at my Faction's rate; the window summary is at the base rate; and "flight now"
does not agree with either.
*Options:* (a) print every window figure at the reading Faction's rate and take "flight
now" from the same source as the table; (b) drop "flight now" and mark the table's numbers
cheap or dear.
*Trade-off:* none I can see for (a) - this reads as a straight presentation defect.

**8. A Nation State below about gdp 10 pays no Ducats at all.**
Observed: 6 Ducats after 25 turns from South Asia, 0 after 20, against 6 a turn from turn 1
from East Asia.
*Options:* (a) make the figure fractional, or floor every controlled state at 1 Ducat;
(b) leave it and accept that the Trading window belongs to whoever holds East Asia, North
America or Europe.
*Trade-off:* (a) opens Relief, bought Influence and bought Materials for every seat, which
is more decisions; (b) is a legitimate design, but then the Trading window should not be
in the first-turn tutorial text for everyone.

**9. Every third turn has nothing to buy.**
Observed game 1 turns 5, 8 and 19: 12, 19 and 21 Materials against a cheapest useful
building of 15, 21 and 25.
*Options:* (a) let Materials be spent part-way into a build so every turn advances
something; (b) add one or two cheap buildings worth buying with change.
*Trade-off:* (a) removes the "save up for it" feeling entirely; (b) lengthens an already
long build list.

**10. The Mass Driver, the Trade Post and the Refuel order never came up once.**
Observed across three games: none built, none used, and I ended with 36, 55 and 88 spare
Fuel. Nothing that saves Fuel is worth 26-35 Materials when Fuel is not scarce.
*Options:* (a) make Fuel genuinely scarce - a smaller tank, or Refineries that want a
Fuel-lean state; (b) accept that these belong to other Factions and other lines of play.
*Trade-off:* (a) would give the tank its teeth and make the whole Propulsion branch matter,
but it makes a bad early game worse; (b) means a good part of 0.06.0's new content is
invisible from this seat.

## Bugs, and rules I could not find explained

**A. A landing that collides with another Faction's landing vanishes with no Report line.**
Game 2, turn 15. The order priced and passed:

    line 1: ok `unload 18 8 into slot mars 3` costs free

The turn's Report then said **nothing at all** about my ship. The only clue was in the
rival summary:

    [Rival] The Custodians ... unloaded at Mars slot 4 ... and founded Hellas Planitia on Mars.

and the ship list afterwards:

    ship 18  Colony Ship  seat 0 at Mars   tank 10/30 | colonists 12

In `engine/src/resolution.rs` (around line 1409) the unload `continue`s on a taken slot
without reporting. A player who did not diff the ship list would not know why nothing
happened.

**B. The Report header reads "turn 0" on the winning turn.** Game 1, turn 25:

    === REPORT, turn 0 ===
    HEADLINE: The Arkwrights founded a Colony in slot 5 on Mars with 16 Colonists.
    ...
    === TURN 25 of 36 (January 2034) ===
    The Arkwrights win: met its Victory Condition.

**C. An Event that takes a station's Modules offline silently blocks shipbuilding.**
Game 3, turn 8. A Grid Failure card hit the Orbital Reef, and:

    line 1: REFUSED `build ship colony:16 colonys`: no Shipyard here

The Shipyard was standing; it was offline. The message says it does not exist. On a
different turn that would have cost me the turn-7 Mars window and thirteen turns.

**D. The AI repeats an impossible Emigrant send, every turn, for turns on end.**
Game 1, turns 10-16, in nearly every Report:

    [Note] 8 Emigrants from Europe found no room in Antarctica and came home.
    [Note] 4 Emigrants from Australia and Oceania found no room in Antarctica and came home.
    [Note] 8 Emigrants from Sub-Saharan Africa found no room in Antarctica and came home.

Three AI seats sending people to an Antarctic Colony with no Habitat room, repeatedly,
without ever building a Habitat. It also fills the human player's Report with noise.

**E. Rules I could not find written down anywhere**
1. **A Colony founded by a Colony Ship or by sea comes with a free Habitat.** It is in the
   code (`modules: vec![Module::new(ModuleKind::Habitat)]`) and it is load-bearing - it is
   why 12 Colonists can be dropped on a bare slot. CONTEXT.md says only that a Colony is
   "founded when a Colony Ship unloads Colonists into a free Colony Slot".
2. **Unloading into a free Colony Slot is not capacity-checked; unloading into an existing
   Colony is.** Two different rules on one order, neither stated.
3. **A station over Earth can be taken by Influence like anything else.** CONTEXT.md says
   it in one clause; PLAYTEST.txt, which is what a player reads, never mentions it, and
   the board gives no way to see it coming.
4. **What an Energy shortfall does.** I found out by doing it (game 2, turn 5):
   "Arkwrights: Energy ran short; shut down Shipyard." One building, engine's choice, one
   turn. CONTEXT.md mentions "shut down (that is the Energy shortfall rule)" only inside a
   list of words to avoid.
5. **Slot indices are 0-based in orders and 1-based in Reports** - `slot earth 1` produces
   "founded a Colony in slot 2".
6. **Whether an arriving Ship may unload on its arrival turn.** It may; `arrived_this_turn`
   blocks only a transit. The message "arrived this turn; it may act next turn" reads as
   though it blocks everything.
