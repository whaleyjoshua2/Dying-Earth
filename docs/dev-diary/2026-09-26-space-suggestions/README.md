# 2026-09-26: suggestions for enriching space and colonization

The designer, going afk during version 0.09.2: *"fan out 4 opus agents to make suggestions about
enriching the space/colonization aspect of the game and then bring the suggestions to me, I will
pick which ones I want to add to the plan/roadmap."*

Four agents read the glossary, the specs to 0.09.2, the data files and the 0.09.0 playtest, each
from one angle, and wrote back. Nothing here is decided, built or ticketed; every figure is a
placeholder for the designer to set. Each item carries the agent's size of the lift. Items are
numbered S7 to S27 so a pick can name them.

**What all four agents found, independently:**

- **Half of two Victory Conditions is free.** A Colonist on a station over Earth counts as off Earth,
  so the Custodians' and Prospectors' 12 can be reached by turn 6 or 7 for about 42 Materials and
  no ship. Three of the four agents proposed the same cure (S2, S16). It is also the oldest fog on
  the 0.09.2 map.
- **A Colony never grows.** Every Colonist off Earth was carried there. Three agents proposed
  growth (S1, S24).
- **Space has almost no voice.** No Choice Card is about the frontier; the space Moments are four;
  the chronicle says nothing about what was built beyond Earth (S19, S21, S22, S25, S27).
- **Ships are buses.** Orbital Battles happened in 7 games of 80; the Prospectors' winning line
  never builds a ship.

## The colonization loop

| | suggestion | lift |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **Born Off Earth: Colonies grow.** A ground Colony with 8+ Colonists and a free working berth gains one Colonist every few turns, at the Colony's own Education Level. A Colony stops being a warehouse and becomes something that lives; a spare Habitat becomes an investment; it helps the Archivists, whose Uploads reached 4 of 12. Adds a source of Colonists beside Pioneers (the Colonist entry would need a clause). *Risk:* shortens the Arkwrights' Diaspora; the 12 bar is already met early; measure before choosing the rate. | small (a counter and a line; the computer wants spare berths) |
| **S2** | **The ground counts.** The Custodians' and Prospectors' 12 off Earth must include some on another Body's ground (say 4 of 12), or a station over Earth counts at half. Closes the free half of the Condition; makes one real voyage mandatory for every Faction; makes the first-to-a-Body race one everyone runs. The Arkwrights' Diaspora already excludes stations over Earth. *Risk:* the Custodians win 6 of 80 already; a sweep decides. | tiny rule; small for the computer (it must plan a voyage) |
| **S3** | **Land the Ship: break a Colony Ship into a Habitat.** At a founding, the Colony Ship may be broken up into the Colony's first Habitat, built at once; everyone aboard lands instead of all but four waiting. A real fork at the game's best moment: keep the hull for a second run, or commit it to this world. Fixes the Arkwrights carrying twelve and landing four. *Collision:* a Ship "is not consumed on arrival". *Risk:* check it is not a cheaper Habitat. | small |
| **S4** | **Depot Colonies: a Refinery refuels its own low orbit.** A ground Colony with a working Refinery refuels its Faction's Ships in its low orbit from the Stockpile, as a station does. The Mars system becomes logistics, not only score; Phobos and Deimos get a reason to be settled beyond the windfall. *Collision:* a station is "the one place a Ship can refuel"; a Blockade in low orbit would shut the depot too. *Risk:* weakens the case for stations beyond Earth. | small |
| **S5** | **Landmark Slots.** Three or four named Colony Slots get one clause of their own (the South Pole-Aitken Basin's ice: Refineries +1; Olympus Mons: transits from there 2 Fuel cheaper). Choosing a slot becomes a plan; landing first gains a second prize. *Risk:* each clause is a rule to learn, and a line under the six-line hover ceiling. | small to medium |
| **S6** | **Resettle Aloft: refugees sent to a station.** A Resettle order can name a station over Earth with free room, lifted through a working Launch Site; each whole unit arriving becomes a Colonist. Ties the dying Earth to the colonies: the climate becomes a push, not only a clock. *Collisions:* Coach Class and the Spaceport's per-Pioneer pay need a ruling; incoming refugees' Unrest never lands. *Risk:* a free source of Colonists on hot boards, feeding the free half unless S2 comes too. | medium |

## Ships, travel and the space between

| | suggestion | lift |
|---|---|---|
| **S7** | **Hard Burn: pay Fuel to arrive sooner.** Beside the flying table's quote, a second row: one turn less for extra Fuel from the Tank. A race: two Colony Ships head for Phobos's 20, and the one that burns lands first with a near-empty tank, under the two-Fuel bar, so it holds no orbit and fights at half. Gives Efficient Transit and the Mass Driver a use beyond saving Fuel. *Risk:* if burning is always worth it, every hull arrives dry; the price has to bite. | small |
| **S8** | **Cross-feed: one hull passes Fuel to another.** A Ship spends its turn's order pouring Fuel into another of its Faction's in the same orbit. Rescues a Stranded hull; lets a fleet reach Mars off the window (35 Fuel against a tank of 30); keeps a Blockade on station with a spare hull as a fuel store, which answers "why build a fifth ship". *Collision:* Stranded "lasts until a station stands where the Ship can reach it"; softens 0.09.1's supply-line pressure; "tanker" is on Refuel's avoid list. *Risk:* dissolves the pressure 0.09.1 built; a loss in the pour would keep it dear. | small |
| **S9** | **Survey Before You Settle.** A free slot shows only its Body's average yields, marked unsurveyed, until a Ship of yours spends an order surveying from low orbit; the real yields are revealed to your Faction alone. The game's first exploration; a peacetime job for a cheap Frigate. *Collision:* "a free slot shows what a Colony there would get"; the draw is only ±25%, so the reveal is small unless the spread widens or each Body hides one find. *Risk:* recent versions are about the game saying what it does; hiding cuts against that. | medium |
| **S10** | **Picket: a Frigate's own stance.** A Frigate-only stance that intercepts arrivals into any orbit of its Body, not only its own, paying the usual two Fuel per Battle. The Battleship holds the orbit, the Frigate watches the approaches: two distinct roles. *Collision:* 0.09.0 made Intercept one orbit only, deliberately. *Risk:* two cheap Frigates could close a Body to landings. | tiny to small |
| **S11** | **Wrecks in Orbit.** A hull destroyed in an orbital Battle leaves a wreck worth part of its Materials plus its tank, drifting away after a few turns; any Faction's Ship there may spend an order salvaging it unless a rival holds the orbit. A reason to hold the field after winning; the Salvage Rights card imagines this already. *Risk:* rarity, at 7 games in 80 with an orbital Battle. | small to medium |
| **S12** | **Laid Up: mothball a hull.** A Ship in the orbit of its own station can be laid up: no Energy upkeep, cannot move, fight or hold Control; back in service a turn after it is ordered. A reserve fleet the player can afford, since upkeep punishes building a hull before it is needed. *Collision:* should Shortfall lay hulls up by itself? *Risk:* logistics, not wonder. | small |
| **S13** | **Ceres, the Far Shelf.** A seventh Body beyond Mars with its own window, a leg longer than a full tank without Efficient Transit, a Mass Driver or Cross-feed, rich yields and the largest first-to-a-Body prize. The unknown at the edge of the map. *Collision:* Ceres is not in the ephemeris table; a third transit table; eases Diaspora. *Risk:* the 36-turn clock; few games may reach it. | large |

## The off-Earth economy

| | suggestion | lift |
|---|---|---|
| **S14** | **Trade Posts that pay by distance.** A Trade Post's flat 3 Ducats per other Body held becomes a figure per Body (Moon 2, Venus 4, Mars 5, Phobos and Deimos 6). A money reason to go far; it is Ducat income, so it reaches the Prospectors' Fund; the Exchange follows. *Risk:* the Prospectors already win 28 of 80; measure with the Fund bar where it is. | tiny |
| **S15** | **Discoveries that stay.** Rich Seam, Ice Deposit and Helium-3 Vein stop being two-turn doublings and raise one slot's yield for good, on a free slot too: a lure, or a reason to spend the doubled Influence on a rival's Colony. The map gets a memory. *Collision:* Deep Mining's and Automated Refining's "×3" texts. *Risk:* three single cards a game, each could decide one on the draw. | small |
| **S16** | **Off-world Presence means another world.** The same cure as S2 from the economy's side: a Colonist on a station over Earth counts half or not at all for the second Victory part; keep the change to the Victory count only. *Risk:* the Custodians' 6 of 80; Mars is unreachable off its window; consider the Moon counting in full. | tiny rule; medium for the computer |
| **S17** | **Small-world shipyards.** Build Where You Dig, which excludes Ships, reaches Ships built at a Shipyard on the Moon, Phobos or Deimos when that Colony has a working Mine. The Moon becomes the fleet yard; a Phobos yard builds hulls already in the Mars system, the one real answer to the window problem. *Risk:* cheaper warships and Missile Carriers everywhere; limit it to Colony Ships and Carriers. | tiny |
| **S18** | **Freight: a Trade Post ships its Colony's ore home.** Each turn a Trade Post sells a share of its own Colony's Mine and Refinery output at the market price, paid as income. A mining Colony gets a money purpose; it reaches the Fund; a steady seller in the market. *Collision:* "Ducats got by selling are not income" needs a stated exception. *Risk:* revives the Moon-Mine engine in Ducats; the one-Module-per-Colonist cap is the brake. | small to medium |
| **S19** | **Choice Cards for Colonies.** Three or four cards that speak to a Colony (an ore buyer at Gateway: sell this turn's Moon output at double, or keep it; a Colony council asks for an Institute). A voice for the Colonies, mostly rows from the existing effect vocabulary; they join on turn 12. *Risk:* dead for a seat with no Colony; the deck is forty distinct cards, so each replaces one or the deck grows. | small |
| **S20** | **Paid Refuel: a station as a port.** Under a Refuel Accord, the partner who draws Fuel pays the market price in Ducats. A station at Mars becomes a toll-house on the long route; a Blockade of a port becomes an economic act. *Risk:* the computer seats rarely use Accords; a price could make them refuse ones they now accept. | small |

## Story and stakes off Earth

| | suggestion | lift |
|---|---|---|
| **S21** | **The chronicle's "Beyond Earth" block, and the survivors' line on Collapse.** One row per Body: who settled it first and when, how many live there at the end and whose they are. A Collapse opens with *"Earth failed at +3.1 C in May 2035. 41 million people lived beyond it: 18 on Mars, 15 on the Moon, 8 in orbit."* Gives the ending the meaning the title implies; the dates exist already. *Risk:* an epilogue that consoles; it should state figures. | small |
| **S22** | **Frontier Choice Cards.** Three or four cards only a seat holding a place off Earth is asked, joining on turn 12: Lava Tube (a free Habitat, or +15 Research), Homesick (a Colonist returns home and Standing rises, or the Colony makes half for a turn), Signal from the Surface (Relations +3 with all, or +20 Materials). The first time a Colony's people want something. *Collision:* the forty-card deck; Homesick moves the Colonist bars. *Risk:* the computer needs a rule for answering each. | small |
| **S23** | **Three missing space Moments.** The first Upload; milestones of the space population (10, 25, 50 million off Earth, table-wide, once each); the first crossing to the Mars system by anyone. Moments that belong to the table. *Risk:* Moment fatigue under the two-a-turn cap; rank them low. | tiny to small |
| **S24** | **Colonies grow their own people, with "the first born on Mars".** S1 from the story's side, with the first birth on each Body a Moment. Suggested in the 2026-09-09 diary and never built; its old gating by a Habitat yield is out of date. *Collision:* every Colonist-counting Victory part; Coach Class loses some point. *Risk:* the bars would probably have to rise. | medium |
| **S25** | **A Colony's own history on its card.** Three to five dated lines: *"Founded March 2033 by the Arkwrights with 8 Colonists. First on Mars. Bombarded July 2034. Taken by the Prospectors January 2035."* Places with pasts. *Collision:* the designer once chose a summary chronicle over dated records; this is narrower but the same question. *Risk:* reading on Colony cards; the six-line hover ceiling. | small to medium |
| **S26** | **Each Faction lands in its own words.** The Colony-founded and first-to-a-Body Moments get one variant per Faction, echoing its motto (the Arkwrights: *"Twelve more who will not go back."*). *Collision:* the voice-budget gate in the 2026-09-15 diary. | tiny |
| **S27** | **Danger with a face.** Dust Storm and Moonquake become Choice Cards: *"A dust storm is bearing down on Hellas Planitia. Evacuate to orbit (hold one Ship, the Colony makes nothing this turn) or ride it out (one Module there may be lost)?"* Space becomes dangerous in a way the player takes part in; the deck count is unchanged. *Risk:* losing a Module is permanent where the old card was a one-turn loss; a harsher deck. | small |

## Where they overlap

- **The free half of the Condition**: S2 and S16 are one idea; S6 and S1/S24 make it worse unless it is taken.
- **Colony growth**: S1 and S24 are one idea from two sides.
- **Cards for the frontier**: S19, S22 and S27 draw on the same forty-card deck; taking several means growing the deck or retiring cards.
- **Fuel and reach**: S7, S8, S4, S17 and S13 all bear on the same problem, that Mars is closed off its window; any one of them changes the others' worth.
- **Things already on the 0.09.2 map**: the Victory bar's band for Colonists in transit (#373) and the stranded Ship (#375) touch S8 and S4.

## Considered and left out by the agents

Naming Colonies (flavour, cut before as #271); per-world stockpiles (the Stockpile entry forbids them); Widgets carried between worlds; a launch-window almanac (the map's hover already says it); Venus as an Energy Body (already so); counting Colonists aboard toward Presence (a Victory question, on the map's fog).
